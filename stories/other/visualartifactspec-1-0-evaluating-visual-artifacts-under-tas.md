Visual artifact tasks test agents' ability to translate task specifications and source materials into outputs that meet practical needs. Evaluation across artifact families helps distinguish broadly applicable authoring capabilities from strengths confined to a particular medium. We introduce VisualArtifactSpec 1.0 (VAS), a benchmark of 211 creation and modification tasks spanning presentations, static graphics, animations and videos, and web interfaces. Each task specifies authoring objectives, source materials, constraints, and deliverables. Existing benchmarks provide limited support for systematically evaluating how authoring choices suit different audiences, purposes, and media across artifact families. VAS evaluates two aspects separately: Task Fulfillment verifies explicit requirements and applicable functionality, while Artifact Performance assesses Content, Organization, Visuals, and Delivery. Task-specific rating anchors instantiate shared criteria for the intended audience, purpose, and medium, with judgments grounded in source materials and artifact inspection records. We evaluate nine model-harness configurations on all 211 tasks. Overall scores range from 42.12 to 70.52, with Artifact Performance accounting for 73.4% of the gap between the highest- and lowest-scoring configurations.
