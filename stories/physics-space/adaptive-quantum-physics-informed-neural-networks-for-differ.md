Physics-informed neural networks solve partial differential equations by combining neural networks with physics constraints, but struggle with accuracy on complex, high-dimensional problems. This work introduces Quantum PINNs enhanced through adaptive collocation point sampling and loss-aware attention mechanisms. The framework dynamically prioritizes regions with large PDE residuals or steep solution gradients, mitigating spectral bias. A trainable loss-weighting scheme balances physics residuals, boundary conditions, and data fidelity. Testing on benchmark fluid flows and reaction-diffusion systems shows the hybrid quantum-classical approach yields at least 60% accuracy improvement in specific regimes. The key insight: optimization bottlenecks limit quantum circuit expressivity, so improvements focus on learning efficiency rather than just model capacity.
