Researchers discovered that molecular glue degraders targeting HuR, an RNA-binding protein frequently dysregulated in cancer cells, show therapeutic potential against BRAF-mutant colorectal cancers. These degraders selectively destroy proteins supporting cancer cell survival and proliferation. This targeted protein destruction strategy addresses cancers harboring specific genetic mutations, offering precision medicine applications. The findings expand the cancer therapy arsenal and demonstrate how understanding protein dependencies in mutant cells enables rational drug design tailored to specific tumor profiles.
