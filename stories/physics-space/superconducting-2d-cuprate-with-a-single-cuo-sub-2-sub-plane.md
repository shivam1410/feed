Researchers successfully fabricated a pristine monolayer cuprate superconductor containing a single CuO2 plane—a major technical achievement. Using this ultra-clean structure, they identified two distinct quantum critical phenomena occurring between the Mott insulating and superconducting phases. Cuprate superconductors are renowned for extraordinarily high critical temperatures, but their mechanisms remain incompletely understood. This single-plane system provides an exceptional experimental platform for studying fundamental quantum phase transitions. These critical phenomena advance understanding of how superconductivity emerges from insulating states, with implications for discovering new high-temperature superconductors and fundamental condensed-matter physics.
