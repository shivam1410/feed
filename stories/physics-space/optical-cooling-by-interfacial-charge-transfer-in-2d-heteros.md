Researchers demonstrated optical cooling in two-dimensional semiconductor heterostructures through phonon-assisted interfacial charge transfer—a process where photons interact with atomic boundaries to remove heat. The approach enables thermal management without cryogenic systems or stringent quantum-efficiency requirements, eliminating a major practical barrier. This breakthrough could expand deployment of 2D semiconductors and quantum devices by removing obstacles that have prevented cost-effective real-world applications in temperature-sensitive systems.
