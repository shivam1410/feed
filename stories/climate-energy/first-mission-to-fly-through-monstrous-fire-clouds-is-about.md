Severe wildfires in France and Oregon create towering pyrocumulus clouds that ascend to extreme altitudes with documented global atmospheric consequences. These formations transport smoke and particulates so high they spread across continents, affecting air quality thousands of kilometers from their source. Understanding these clouds matters because their aerosols alter atmospheric radiation balance and climate patterns globally. Researchers are launching an unprecedented mission to fly through these formations and measure their physical properties, data that will substantially improve climate models' ability to predict wildfire impacts on Earth's atmosphere.
