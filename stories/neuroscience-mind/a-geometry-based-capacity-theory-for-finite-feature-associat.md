Researchers built a mathematical theory explaining how associative memory stores and retrieves information based on the geometry of its features. The theory splits retrieval errors into two types: noise that shrinks as dimensions grow, and structural interference that never goes away. They tested it on images and showed when adding more features helps versus when you need to redesign the representation itself.
