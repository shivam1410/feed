AI agents can handle research engineering but fail at open-ended research questions, according to shadow evaluations conducted on two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 papers. Frontier AI agents received six days and thousands of dollars in computational resources to tackle the central research questions posed in each paper. Agents completed all engineering work without human help, successfully executing code, building systems, and solving technical problems. However, agents made no substantial progress toward answering the core research questions; both papers were unambiguously rejected by their original authors. Five recurring failure modes emerged across evaluations: poor judgment about research quality standards, uncreative problem-solving responses to design shortcomings, ineffective recovery from dead ends, poor resource awareness, and instruction drift over time. Results demonstrate that current frontier agents can handle engineering components of research but lack the creativity, judgment, and strategic thinking required for pioneering research.
