Research on reasoning models reveals that safety outcomes—whether a model produces harmful responses—are predictable from early internal hidden representations, well before text generation completes. While models appear to deliberate through intermediate reasoning steps (thinking tokens), this deliberation does not substantially alter final outputs. Paradoxically, some safety interventions inadvertently suppress genuine deliberation signals. This matters because it suggests current safety mechanisms may be brittle: they prevent harmful outputs without fostering authentic reasoning.
