Researchers propose an MLP-based surrogate model to replace computationally expensive radiative transfer simulations for CO2 retrieval. The feedforward neural network predicts top-of-atmosphere radiances in the CO2 weak band using combined mean absolute error loss on both radiances and radiative transfer Jacobians to preserve spectral accuracy and sensitivity to geophysical parameters. Coupled with the NanoCarb imaging interferometer instrumental response, this efficient surrogate yields a precise forward model showing promising results for CO2 concentration retrieval.
