Researchers engineered probiotics equipped with a synthetic glucose-responsive circuit that transiently colonize the gut and secrete therapeutic factors when glucose levels rise. In mouse and nonhuman primate models, this approach enables oral, sense-and-respond control of diabetes by detecting blood glucose increases and responding with therapeutic secretion. The system temporarily colonizes the gut while providing on-demand therapeutic intervention triggered by the body's own glucose measurements, then clears naturally. This precision medicine strategy avoids permanent bacterial colonization while delivering treatment exactly when needed. The work demonstrates a fundamentally new approach to diabetes management using engineered microorganisms as sense-and-respond therapeutic devices, combining biotechnology with synthetic biology.
