This review examines geometric deep learning (GDL) as an approach to rational multi-target drug design, addressing limitations of traditional structure-based drug design for complex diseases with compensatory signaling pathways. GDL architectures ranging from invariant graph neural networks to SE(3)-equivariant diffusion models process non-Euclidean molecular data to capture 3D structural relationships. Applications span characterization of shared binding pockets via geometric embeddings, multi-target bioactivity prediction through heterogeneous graph fusion, and de novo generation of dual-target ligands. Emerging algorithms integrate diffusion models with reinforcement learning to autonomously resolve geometric conflicts between multiple binding sites.
