Small-molecule drug compounds block β-arrestin proteins from interacting with activated G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs), key cell-surface signaling molecules. Pharmacological and structural studies reveal how inhibitors work at the molecular level and identify a previously unknown allosteric regulatory site on β-arrestins. This allosteric site allows alternative control of β-arrestin function. Understanding the mechanism enables better drug design targeting β-arrestin signaling. β-arrestins regulate diverse cellular processes, and modulating them could treat various diseases. Discovery of this regulatory site opens new precision therapeutic targeting avenues.
