Substantial intraspecific genetic variation, working in concert with species turnover, drives coordinated shifts in drought resistance across tropical forest regions. In drier forests, species exhibit increased embolism resistance—the ability to withstand water stress without vascular failure—and wider stomatal safety margins that enable water conservation. These physiological and genetic adaptations allow species to survive with reduced water availability. Understanding these variation-driven mechanisms is essential for predicting how tropical forests will respond to intensifying droughts driven by climate change and for assessing shifts in carbon storage capacity.
