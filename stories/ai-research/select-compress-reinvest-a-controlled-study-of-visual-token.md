This study examines how multimodal language models allocate limited computational resources when processing extended video sequences. Frame selection—strategically choosing which frames to analyze—emerges as the dominant driver of model accuracy, making it the critical optimization target. Spatial compression of selected frames (reducing resolution) imposes negligible accuracy cost if the computational savings are reinvested into analyzing additional frames. The research highlights that achieving efficient long-video understanding requires both intelligent frame selection and strategic budget reallocation across the selection-compression dimension. The authors propose a unified evaluation harness enabling fair, systematic comparison of different frame selection strategies across models and benchmarks.
