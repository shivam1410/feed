Bacteriophages deploy a viral kinase called T7K as a "loose cannon" enzyme that phosphorylates nearly all bacterial proteins. Despite this broad activity, T7K's specificity for DNA-binding proteins proves strategically important: it preferentially disables bacterial defense systems that target nucleic acids, which represent the primary threat to viral infection. This selective strategy allows the phage to neutralize defenses most relevant to phage DNA while using a chemically indiscriminate enzyme. The discovery illuminates how viruses have evolved elegant solutions to overwhelming host immunity and informs efforts to develop phage-based therapies.
