Scientists identified BACH2 protein as a brake suppressing fetal hemoglobin genes. When BACH2 is removed or weakened, the protein NRF2 binds directly to gene regions and activates fetal hemoglobin. This matters because reactivating fetal hemoglobin could treat sickle cell disease since it naturally resists sickling, and the mechanism is now clear enough for therapeutic development.
