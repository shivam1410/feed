Female treefrogs select mates less carefully when exposed to a chorus of competing males, revealing a cognitive limitation: overwhelming choice degrades decision quality. This behavioral pattern demonstrates that mate selection, even in animals, becomes disrupted by information overload. Results have implications for evolutionary biology and animal behavior, showing how sensory and cognitive systems struggle with competing stimuli. The principle—that abundance of choice doesn't always improve outcomes—may extend beyond biology to other decision-making systems where selective attention faces excessive information.
