Massive wildfires ravaged continental expanses dominated by ferns during the final days of the Triassic period, coinciding with one of Earth's most severe extinction events. The infernos destroyed vast plant ecosystems while land and sea animals experienced simultaneous mass extinction. This research reveals how multiple catastrophic forces—wildfire, climate change, and ecosystem collapse—accumulated simultaneously. Understanding these fire patterns helps scientists model interactions between climate disruption and biotic collapse, suggesting wildfire was a significant component of the Triassic extinction mechanism.
