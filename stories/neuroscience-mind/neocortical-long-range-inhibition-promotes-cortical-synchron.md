In mice, a sparse population of sleep-active, long-range inhibitory neurons in the neocortex drives widespread cortical synchronization promoting sleep. These inhibitory neurons extend connections across broad cortical regions, generating synchronized neural patterns characteristic of sleep states. This discovery identifies a specific neural mechanism regulating sleep at the cortical level. Understanding how these neurons orchestrate sleep-promoting synchronization provides insight into sleep regulation and potential therapeutic targets for sleep disorders.
