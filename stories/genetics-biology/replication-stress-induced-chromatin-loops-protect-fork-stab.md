When DNA replication encounters stress, cells form transient chromatin loops enclosing stalled replication forks enriched with heterochromatin marks. These protective structures represent a cellular safety mechanism maintaining genome stability during adverse conditions. Understanding this mechanism reveals how cells protect genetic information when replication machinery encounters obstacles like DNA damage or nucleotide depletion, preserving genomic integrity under stress.
