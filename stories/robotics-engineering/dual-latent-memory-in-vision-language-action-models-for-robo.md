LaMem-VLA introduces a latent-memory-native framework that integrates historical robot experience directly into vision-language-action reasoning for manipulation tasks. The approach uses coordinated memory components operating in the same latent space as the visual and language encoders, enabling seamless integration of past experience with current perception and action planning. By embedding memory operations natively into the latent space rather than treating them as separate bolt-on modules, the architecture improves reasoning coherence and policy effectiveness. This latent-memory integration enables robots to leverage accumulated experience more effectively when planning and executing complex manipulation tasks, improving both speed and success rates.
