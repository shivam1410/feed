Researchers studied human cells cultured aboard space stations in microgravity and found they produced significantly fewer mitochondrial proteins compared to Earth-cultured controls. Mitochondria are cellular powerhouses responsible for energy production, and reduced protein synthesis likely explains the muscle wasting and weakness astronauts experience during spaceflight. The finding identifies a specific cellular mechanism underlying physiological changes in microgravity. Understanding this mechanism may lead to countermeasures preserving astronaut health during long-duration missions.
