ZimaBlue learns generalizable world action models from large-scale egocentric video using a three-stage curriculum and slow-fast architecture that builds increasingly sophisticated representations. The model substantially improves zero-shot robotic manipulation by learning predictive models of how actions affect the world from human video, without task-specific training. The three-stage approach progressively increases learning difficulty, while the slow-fast architecture balances computational efficiency with model capacity. By learning from egocentric perspective data at scale, ZimaBlue develops representations that transfer to robotic control tasks. Large-scale human video provides valuable supervisory signal for learning world models that generalize to embodied control.
