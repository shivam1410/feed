Humans and bacteria employ evolutionarily ancient, highly conserved mechanisms to defend against viral infections, from detecting pathogens to activating immune responses. This comprehensive review reveals that human antiviral immunity mirrors bacterial defense systems against bacteriophages in fundamental ways. These shared principles have been preserved across billions of years of evolution and across all domains of life, suggesting deep universal rules govern how any host resists viral invasion. The similarities indicate common sensing mechanisms, signaling pathways, and effector strategies that emerged early in life's history and remain essential. These universal principles may guide new therapeutic strategies.
