Three seemingly distinct language model training methods—GRPO, Dr. GRPO, and DAPO—are mathematically shown to be variations of a single approach based on standard deviation adjustment. The unifying principle centers on how disagreement among sampled model outputs determines both learning effectiveness and the magnitude of training updates. By reformulating these methods through the lens of group standard deviation, the paper reveals that apparent algorithmic differences mask underlying mathematical equivalence. This unified perspective clarifies what distinguishes effective training methods at their core: principled adjustment of prediction variance across multiple model samples. Understanding this mathematical foundation enables better design of future training approaches.
