During simultaneous learning of two motor skills, the brain employs a division of labor, according to simultaneous neural imaging in mice. The cerebral cortex generalizes activity patterns across both skills, enabling knowledge transfer and rapid learning. Cerebellar granule cells, meanwhile, reorient their activity trajectories to distinctly separate the skills, preventing confusion between contexts. This complementary organization allows the brain to simultaneously generalize principles across tasks while maintaining precise, context-specific control. The finding explains how the cerebellum supports fine-grained motor learning while the cortex captures broader patterns.
