Scientists discovered how a brain circuit stores heading memories using a mix of slow excitation, fast inhibition, and a disinhibitory gate that controls the network. This design lets the circuit rapidly switch between actively measuring direction and holding that memory stable. Understanding this mechanism reveals how animal brains encode persistent navigational goals.
