Turning on the gene Meis2 in specific brain cells called hippocampal parvalbumin-expressing interneurons rescued lost plasticity in adult mice with neurodevelopmental disorders. This genetic intervention also improved memory formation, normalized neural network function, and eliminated seizures—major symptoms of developmental brain disorders. The findings suggest that neurodevelopmental conditions impair the brain's ability to change and adapt based on experience, a process called plasticity. By restoring plasticity-related genes in adulthood, researchers reversed these deficits, offering a novel therapeutic strategy. This work demonstrates that adult brains retain potential for repair if key plasticity genes are reactivated.
