A study of Alzheimer's disease pathology reveals that neuroinflammation alone may not be necessary to cause early synapse loss—the primary driver of cognitive decline. Aberrant ERBB4 signaling in excitatory neurons appears sufficient to drive synapse loss independent of inflammatory mechanisms. This finding challenges the prevailing view that neuroinflammation is the primary mechanism in early Alzheimer's and suggests targeting neuronal ERBB4 dysfunction may be an alternative therapeutic strategy. Reframing early disease mechanisms could redirect research.
