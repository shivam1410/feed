Teosinte—the wild ancestor of modern cultivated maize—carries genetic alleles that enhance nitrogen assimilation and increase seed protein content when introduced into contemporary maize varieties. Critically, these nutritional improvements occur without compromising crop yield, demonstrating that strategic crosses between ancient wild relatives and modern agriculture can simultaneously enhance both nutrition and productivity. The trait combinations offer a template for practical crop improvement. Why it matters: This represents a powerful and scalable strategy for enhancing agricultural productivity and nutritional value to meet growing global food demands and support nutrition security in coming decades, as both climate pressures and population growth intensify worldwide.
