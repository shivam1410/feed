Researchers identified ZMYND8, a protein that suppresses immune signaling in T cells and drives them toward terminal exhaustion—a state where they lose their ability to fight infection or cancer. The protein works by suppressing IL-2 receptor signaling and blocking p300-mediated activation of the gene encoding the IL-2 receptor alpha chain (Il2ra). By deleting ZMYND8 from CD8+ T cells, scientists restored the cells to more effector-like states, substantially enhancing their antiviral and anti-tumor immunity. This finding suggests that ZMYND8 acts as a molecular brake on T cell activation, and removing it unleashes immune potential. The discovery offers a promising therapeutic avenue for cancer immunotherapy and antiviral treatments, targeting a protein that enforces T cell dysfunction.
