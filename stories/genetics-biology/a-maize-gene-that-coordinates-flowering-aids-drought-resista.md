Male and female maize flower clusters normally flower synchronously, maximizing reproductive success. Drought disrupts this synchrony, causing significant yield losses. Researchers identified the SAUR72 gene as essential for maintaining flowering synchrony under drought stress. The gene preserves coordination ensuring pollen availability when female flowers are receptive, preventing reproductive failure under water stress. This finding offers a practical breeding strategy: selecting or engineering maize varieties with enhanced SAUR72 expression could maintain yields during droughts. As climate change increases drought frequency and intensity, such genetic improvements are vital for food security, allowing crops to maintain productivity.
