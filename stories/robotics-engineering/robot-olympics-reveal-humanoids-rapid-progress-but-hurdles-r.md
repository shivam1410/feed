Humanoid robots made impressive showings at 'Robot Olympics,' breaking records in athletic competitions including sprints and other controlled speed-focused events. However, they encountered significant difficulties with practical, hands-on tasks, particularly struggling to perform basic manual work like hammering nails. This mixed performance encapsulates the current state of humanoid robotics: genuine progress in executing controlled, repetitive athletic movements paired with persistent limitations in complex manipulation and adaptive real-world problem-solving. The results highlight a crucial insight: sophisticated motion control doesn't automatically translate into dexterous, flexible task execution.
