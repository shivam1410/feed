The ARP2/3 protein complex works with actomyosin in an oscillatory—rhythmic back-and-forth—program at the neuronal soma to establish polarity. This oscillatory mechanism drives the asymmetric development that distinguishes a neuron's sending end from receiving ends. Rather than continuous directional force, the process involves timed protein activity cycles that gradually build asymmetry. This discovery illuminates fundamental mechanisms of cell differentiation and reveals how neurons self-organize their internal structure during development.
