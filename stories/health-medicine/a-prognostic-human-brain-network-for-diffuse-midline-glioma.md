Tumor network mapping of diffuse midline glioma, an aggressive pediatric brain cancer, identifies a conserved and prognostically important neural network implicated in disease progression. Rather than creating entirely aberrant pathways, researchers propose that these tumors survive and proliferate by hijacking otherwise normal and healthy brain circuits. This mechanistic insight suggests the cancer exploits fundamental neural organization to support its growth and survival. Understanding the disease's dependence on normal brain networks may enable therapeutic strategies targeting these specific circuits, offering new approaches to treating this devastating childhood cancer with broader implications for other brain malignancies.
