A diet-microbiome-host axis involving commensal-derived acetylcholine strengthens mucosal immune defenses and reinforces host-microbiota mutualism. Gut bacteria produce acetylcholine, which enhances immune education at intestinal mucosal surfaces, training immune cells to respond appropriately to pathogens while tolerating beneficial bacteria. This mechanism explains why a healthy microbiota is essential for protective immunity and demonstrates how bacterial metabolites directly regulate host immune function.
