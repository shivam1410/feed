Mining for energy-transition minerals—cobalt, lithium for batteries and renewables—is driving extensive deforestation across sub-Saharan Africa, paradoxically undermining climate mitigation. Analysis reveals large-scale forest clearing directly linked to mining expansion, with tropical forests serving as irreplaceable carbon sinks and biodiversity repositories. This represents a major climate policy contradiction: minerals essential for clean energy transition are extracted through practices that accelerate deforestation and release stored carbon. The finding highlights urgency for sustainable extraction standards and supply-chain accountability as renewable energy deployment scales. Critical context for understanding hidden climate costs in technology transitions and tensions between energy and ecosystem protection in developing regions.
