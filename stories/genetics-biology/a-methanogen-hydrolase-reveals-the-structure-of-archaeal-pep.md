An enzyme from a dominant human gut bacterium has revealed the hidden structure of archaeal cell walls for the first time. The enzyme, called ArmA, specifically cuts a sugar polymer that researchers had never identified before in archaeal peptidoglycan cell walls. This unexpected and surprising chemistry in everyday gut microbes could fundamentally reshape our understanding of microbial biology and cellular architecture.
