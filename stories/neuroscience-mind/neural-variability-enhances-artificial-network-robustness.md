Cortical neurons exhibit substantial trial-to-trial variability in response to repeated stimuli, unlike more consistent peripheral sensory neurons. This work investigates whether structured noise in artificial neural networks improves robustness to adversarial attacks and natural image modifications using activation covariance to characterize noise structure. Results show structured noise significantly improves robustness, particularly for naturalistic modifications, though noise structure tailored to adversarial attacks generalizes better across attack types. This biological pattern—noise as a feature rather than a liability—offers a plausible strategy for creating robust systems using only local information.
