Observations of an unusual cosmic-dawn source reveal that early supermassive black holes may have grown rapidly while enshrouded in dense gas, which obscures visible light and reddens their appearance. These gas-enshrouded black holes may account for puzzling 'little red dots'—faint, distant objects detected in deep-field observations. The discovery illuminates a growth mechanism for the surprisingly massive black holes in the early universe.
