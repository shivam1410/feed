Analysis of prokaryotic STAND NTPases—immune proteins evolutionarily related to animal and plant immune receptors—reveals that bacteria possess diverse pattern-recognition sensors that detect most core structural and replication proteins of bacteriophages. This systematic characterization shows bacteria have evolved sophisticated antiviral defense mechanisms. These diverse bacterial sensors represent ancient immune strategies that predate and likely inspired more complex animal immunity. Understanding these bacterial defense systems illuminates how cellular immunity evolved across all life and may inspire new approaches to combating phage-resistant bacteria and developing antimicrobial strategies based on natural defenses.
