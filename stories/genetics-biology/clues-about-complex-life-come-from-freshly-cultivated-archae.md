Scientists have grown two archaea strains in the lab. They belong to a lineage close to the ancestor of complex cells with membrane-bound organelles. The microbes have simple internal structure but elaborate surfaces, and they live in partnerships with other microbes. Their traits hint at how eukaryotic cells arose. It matters because living relatives give researchers something real to study about that early step.
