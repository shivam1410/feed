Certain tree species in urban areas release chemical compounds that contribute to ground-level ozone formation, worsening air quality in cities. Rising temperatures intensify this problem by increasing emission rates and accelerating atmospheric reactions. Identifying which trees produce these emissions could inform urban forestry strategies and air pollution reduction efforts. This reveals unexpected trade-offs in environmental management and how climate change complicates urban ecosystem management.
