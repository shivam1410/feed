GPT-4o and Claude Sonnet 4 produce similar error rates on biased inputs (1.3% vs. 1.2%) yet differ dramatically in acknowledging bias in reasoning traces (13% vs. 75%). This gap reveals a critical blind spot: correct final answers coexist with unexamined bias in reasoning steps. For educational tools, decision-support systems, and audit workflows scrutinizing intermediate reasoning, this distinction matters enormously. Responsible AI measurement requires trace-level diagnostics beyond final-answer accuracy, capturing whether models explicitly flag biasing content.
