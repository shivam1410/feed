A proximity-mapping atlas identifies tumor-associated proximity antigens and defines spatial relationships between membrane proteins in disease contexts. Analysis revealed EGFR–CDCP1 as a co-localized pair whose simultaneous targeting enhances tumor killing by multispecific therapeutics compared to single-target approaches. By mapping protein neighborhoods, researchers discovered how spatial organization drives immunotherapy efficacy. This proximity-based strategy expands therapeutic targets beyond high-expression proteins to spatially organized antigen pairs, potentially improving cancer treatment outcomes and informing rational design of combination immunotherapies.
