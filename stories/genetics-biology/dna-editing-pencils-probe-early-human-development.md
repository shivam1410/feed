Adenine base editing, a genome-rewriting tool, offers researchers a precise method to investigate gene function in early human embryos. This pencil approach to DNA editing allows scientists to make targeted changes to genetic sequences, essentially probing how specific genes control development during the earliest stages of human life. Unlike traditional gene editing that permanently alters DNA, base editing provides a cleaner approach with fewer off-target effects. By applying this technique to embryos, researchers can determine which genes are necessary for specific developmental processes, revealing fundamental mechanisms of human development and potentially informing treatments for developmental disorders.
