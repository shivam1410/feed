Scientists discovered that embryonic 'organizer cells' can direct body development across diverse animal species. When transplanted between embryos of different phyla, these organizing cells instruct embryos on what body type to construct. This finding suggests universal genetic signals exist across the tree of life—common developmental instructions regulating body assembly in animals separated by evolutionary history. The discovery hints at fundamental mechanisms underlying animal evolution and development, implying early body patterns are controlled by conserved signaling systems that appeared before modern animals diversified.
