Non-native pine trees from the Northern Hemisphere grow significantly faster and withstand drought much better when transplanted to the Southern Hemisphere. This counterintuitive advantage suggests they escape ecological constraints limiting performance in native regions. Northern Hemisphere trees face fundamental trade-offs between rapid growth and drought tolerance—investing in one leaves fewer resources for the other. In the Southern Hemisphere's different climate and conditions, non-native pines apparently avoid this growth-stress-tolerance trade-off, achieving both rapid growth and superior drought resilience simultaneously.
