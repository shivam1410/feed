Experiments demonstrate a previously underappreciated corrosion mechanism: spontaneously charged water droplets can damage protective coatings on metals and trigger accelerated corrosion. When water droplets develop electric charge—a phenomenon observed to occur naturally—they become electrochemically aggressive toward underlying metals, degrading protective surface coatings. This mechanism expands understanding of environmental corrosion beyond traditional chemical degradation pathways. The finding has practical implications for protecting materials in humid environments, from aircraft structures to bridge infrastructure to outdoor equipment. Understanding charge development in water droplets informs design of more resilient protective coatings.
