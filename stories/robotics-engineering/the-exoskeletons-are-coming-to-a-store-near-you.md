Exoskeletons enabling walking, running, and jumping are transitioning from research laboratories to commercial consumer markets, with initial deployments on hiking trails marking the start of mainstream adoption. These wearable robotic devices represent a convergence of biomechanics engineering and advanced materials science aimed at enhancing human mobility. The shift toward commercial viability matters significantly because it could provide meaningful benefits to people with mobility limitations and injuries, potentially restoring independence and enabling greater physical activity. The technology's movement to hiking trails suggests it has achieved sufficient maturity, safety validation, and cost-effectiveness for public use.
