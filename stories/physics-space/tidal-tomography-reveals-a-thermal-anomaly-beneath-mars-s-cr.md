Analysis of time-variable gravity field data from Mars orbiters reveals internal structure beneath the Martian crustal dichotomy—the stark division between younger northern lowlands and ancient southern highlands. These measurements indicate a 200–400°C thermal anomaly persists in the present-day mantle beneath the southern highlands. This thermal preservation suggests that despite billions of years of cooling, internal heat sources or slow cooling rates have maintained elevated temperature. Understanding subsurface thermal structure constrains models of Mars's geologic evolution.
