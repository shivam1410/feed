Crystal structures of an enzyme that catalyzes glycolipid synthesis—sugar-carrying lipids essential for cell signaling and structure—reveal an unexpected reaction mechanism and built-in regulatory brake that evolved specifically in primates. This discovery illuminates how enzyme regulation has diverged during primate evolution, suggesting primate-specific modifications to glycolipid synthesis may confer functional advantages for primate neural or immune function. Glycolipids are particularly important in brain tissue and immune cells, where their structural diversity influences signal transduction and cellular communication. Understanding primate-specific evolutionary modifications to glycolipid chemistry may reveal fundamental differences in how primate brains operate.
