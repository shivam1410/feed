The locus coeruleus contains topographically organized noradrenaline neurons forming distinct dorsal and ventral subpopulations that play different functional roles in flexible learning. These neurons have different projection patterns and their activity encodes choice switching behavior, reward-prediction errors when outcomes diverge from expectations, and disregard of cues that stop predicting rewards. By organizing into functionally distinct anatomical regions, locus coeruleus neurons support adaptive learning where animals switch strategies when environments change and old patterns no longer work. This anatomical organization explains how the brain implements flexible behavioral adaptation, enabling organisms to abandon unsuccessful strategies and adopt new ones when circumstances warrant. The topographic structure directly enables behavioral flexibility.
