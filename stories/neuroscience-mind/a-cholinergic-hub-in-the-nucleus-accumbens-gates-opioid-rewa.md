A cholinergic hub in the nucleus accumbens controls whether opioids become rewarding and addiction-prone. Blocking opioid receptors on acetylcholine-producing interneurons abolished morphine reward acquisition without impairing pain relief—the drug still works as a painkiller but doesn't trigger addiction-driving reward signals. This mechanism decouples morphine's analgesic effects from dopamine surges. Pro-cholinergic strategies could limit opioid-reward learning and addiction risk while preserving pain control, opening a therapeutic angle for preventing addiction without sacrificing therapeutic benefits.
