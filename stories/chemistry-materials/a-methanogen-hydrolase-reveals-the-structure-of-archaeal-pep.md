An enzyme from Methanobrevibacter smithii (a dominant gut microbe) breaks down archaeal cell walls and reveals an unexpected chemical structure. The microbe cleaves a previously unknown sugar in archaeal peptidoglycan. This discovery surprises because archaea were thought to have different cell walls than bacteria. This matters because understanding gut microbe biology at a molecular level reveals the surprising sophistication of organisms living inside human bodies.
