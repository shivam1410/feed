A well-preserved Late Cretaceous fossil snake from Brazil demonstrates exceptional brain and ecological diversity among the earliest snakes. The specimen reveals that ancient snakes displayed significant variation in brain morphology and occupied diverse ecological niches much earlier than previously documented. By examining the fossil's cranial anatomy and physical characteristics, paleontologists inferred different ecological strategies employed by early snakes. This discovery indicates that neural and behavioral complexity evolved rapidly after snakes diverged from lizards. Understanding early snake diversity and brain structure provides insights into sensory evolution, behavioral ecology, and how developmental changes in body plan drove the evolution of novel ecological roles.
