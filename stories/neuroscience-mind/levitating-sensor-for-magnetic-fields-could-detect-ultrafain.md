A simple levitating sensor can detect ultrafaint magnetic fields generated by brain activity, potentially rivaling much more complex conventional brain imaging systems. The device's elegant, minimalist design overcomes limitations of existing neuroscience equipment through clever physics. This simple approach could advance brain imaging research and neuroscience applications. This matters because simpler, more accessible alternatives to expensive complex equipment could democratize neuroscience research, reduce costs, and potentially enable new applications like searches for dark matter signatures that weren't possible before.
