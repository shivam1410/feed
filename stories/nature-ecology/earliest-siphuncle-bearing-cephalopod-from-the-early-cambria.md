Eoceras shaanxiense from early Cambrian rocks is the earliest known cephalopod preserving septa and a segmented tube resembling a primordial siphuncle. Chambered shells facilitate buoyancy regulation, allowing organisms to move vertically in water. The fossil extends cephalopod origins backward in time and reveals how early cephalopods assembled the specialized shell architecture enabling exploitation of new ecological niches. This discovery provides crucial evidence for reconstructing the evolutionary trajectory leading to modern cephalopods and illuminating how key innovations emerged in marine life.
