Reinforcement learning for molecular design optimizes molecular properties but ignores prediction uncertainty, leading to exploration of uncertain regions with unreliable scores. This work proposes two approaches: treating uncertainty as an optimization objective alongside scoring functions to trade exploitation against reliability, or using uncertainty to modulate policy updates reducing focus on poorly-supported molecules. Testing across controlled models and real-world molecular design tasks demonstrates more reliable optimization. Uncertainty awareness reduces synthesis and testing failures.
