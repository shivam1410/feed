A well-preserved fossil snake from Late Cretaceous deposits in Brazil demonstrates that early snakes exhibited exceptional morphological and ecological diversity, contrary to prior assumptions that early snakes were morphologically uniform and ecologically specialized. The specimen reveals specialized brain structures alongside compelling evidence of varied ecological roles and environmental niches occupied by early snakes. This fossil provides rare and direct insight into early snake evolution during their rapid diversification and adaptive radiation. Understanding brain morphology evolution in early snakes illuminates how ecological specialization and neural complexity co-evolved and diversified. This paleontological discovery challenges oversimplified narratives of snake evolutionary origins and demonstrates that early snakes occupied a broader and more diverse ecological spectrum than previously appreciated by the scientific community.
