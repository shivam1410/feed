Recent advances address training accurate models with incomplete, inexact, or inaccurate supervision—a common real-world constraint where perfect annotation is infeasible. The field explores new supervision paradigms including confidence-difference binary classification with consistent approaches, complementary-label learning in multi-class settings with relaxed data-generation assumptions, and partial-label learning with standardized evaluation frameworks. These methods reduce reliance on perfectly annotated datasets while maintaining theoretical consistency guarantees. This matters because most real applications lack high-quality labeled data; enabling learning from weak supervision makes deep learning deployable in practical settings where annotation is costly, data is noisy, or labels are incomplete.
