Electrical stimulation of the human temporal cortex activates distinct, cell-type-specific gene programs operating in tandem with neural network activity. Different neuronal and glial cell types respond transcriptionally to identical stimulation stimulus, indicating that stimulation engages multiple functional pathways simultaneously. This cell-type-specific response could explain how brain stimulation slows cognitive decline. Understanding which genes activate in which cell types enables optimization of stimulation parameters, frequency, and anatomical targets to maximize therapeutic benefit. These insights inform development of more effective neuromodulation therapies for neurodegenerative diseases.
