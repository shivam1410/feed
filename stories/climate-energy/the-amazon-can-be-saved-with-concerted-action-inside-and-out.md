Deforestation in the Amazon is declining, and new evidence demonstrates the rainforest can withstand global warming—but only with sustained worldwide effort to stop cutting it down. The research indicates that preventing deforestation through coordinated action inside and outside Brazil is essential. Key to this is recognizing that the Amazon's ability to resist climate impacts depends on halting cutting, not merely slowing deforestation. The findings matter because they show the ecosystem's survival is possible, but hinges on international commitment. Without worldwide action to end logging, the rainforest will lose resilience to climate stress, making global coordination crucial for preservation.
