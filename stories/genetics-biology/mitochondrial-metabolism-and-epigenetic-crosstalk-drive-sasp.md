In senescent (aging) cells, mitochondria produce acetyl-CoA, which drives histone modifications that activate inflammatory genes—part of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP). Blocking SLC25A1, a mitochondrial transporter, reduces this process. The discovery links mitochondrial metabolism to age-related inflammation through epigenetics, suggesting that inhibiting SLC25A1 or related pathways could suppress pro-inflammatory programs in aging cells and delay age-related functional decline.
