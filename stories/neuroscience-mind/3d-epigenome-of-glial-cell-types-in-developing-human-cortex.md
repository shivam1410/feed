Three-dimensional epigenomic profiling of four glial cell types in the developing human cortex reveals cell-type-specific regulatory elements and chromatin interactions. The analysis shows how different glial types distinctly organize their DNA and control genes. These findings illuminate how non-coding genetic variants linked to neuropsychiatric diseases—schizophrenia, autism—may work through cell-type-specific mechanisms. The research also clarifies uniquely human brain evolution. Understanding glial epigenomics is crucial because these cells profoundly influence neural development and function, providing a foundation for understanding how genetic risk factors translate into brain disorders.
