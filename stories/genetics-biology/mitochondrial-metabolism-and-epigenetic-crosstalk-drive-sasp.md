In senescent cells, mitochondria produce acetyl-CoA that drives increased histone acetylation and chromatin accessibility at inflammatory gene loci, promoting a pro-inflammatory state linked to age-related functional decline. Inhibiting the SLC25A1 protein that exports acetyl-CoA from mitochondria reduces these inflammatory effects, revealing a mitochondrial-epigenetic crosstalk mechanism with therapeutic potential. Targeting mitochondrial metabolism and its epigenetic consequences could delay age-related functional decline.
