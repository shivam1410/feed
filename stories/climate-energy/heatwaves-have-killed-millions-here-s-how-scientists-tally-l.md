Scientists employ two distinct methodologies to estimate deaths from heatwaves such as those currently affecting parts of Asia and Europe. These different counting methods—analyzing excess mortality and heat-attribution models—produce varying estimates of heatwave fatalities, creating challenges for standardizing public health assessments. The variation in mortality counts highlights how methodology affects understanding of heat-related health impacts. This matters because accurate tallying directly informs public health policy responses and helps governments understand the true human cost of extreme heat events for resource allocation.
