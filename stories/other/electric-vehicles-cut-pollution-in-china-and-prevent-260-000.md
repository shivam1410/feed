Widespread electric vehicle adoption in China prevented approximately 260,000 premature deaths by substantially reducing air pollution from fossil-fuel combustion. However, detailed analysis reveals variable effectiveness across different pollutant types—certain harmful emissions declined significantly due to reduced petroleum consumption, while other major pollutants remain largely unaffected by the vehicle transition. These findings highlight both substantial public health benefits of vehicular electrification and its notable limitations in addressing all air quality concerns simultaneously.
