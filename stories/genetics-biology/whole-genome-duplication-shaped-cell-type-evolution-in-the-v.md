Comparative analyses of single-cell transcriptomes across diverse vertebrate species—including humans, mice, lizards, lampreys, and amphioxus—reveal that whole-genome duplication events profoundly shaped vertebrate brain complexity. Duplicated genes, called ohnologues, proved essential for the diversification of neuronal and glial cell types during early vertebrate evolution. These gene duplicates acquired specialized functions, allowing vertebrate brains to develop hundreds of distinct cell types instead of the limited diversity seen in invertebrate nervous systems. The research explains how two ancient rounds of genome duplication enabled the sophistication of vertebrate neural circuits, connecting major evolutionary transitions to molecular mechanisms.
