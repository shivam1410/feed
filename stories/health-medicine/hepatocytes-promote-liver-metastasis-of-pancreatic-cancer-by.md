Pancreatic cancer cells metastasizing to the liver lack the enzyme for serine synthesis, so they reprogram neighboring hepatocytes to produce this amino acid instead, promoting tumor growth and survival. This reveals metabolic cooperation where cancer hijacks host metabolism to sustain metastatic growth. Disrupting this cross-talk could potentially starve metastatic tumors of essential nutrients.
