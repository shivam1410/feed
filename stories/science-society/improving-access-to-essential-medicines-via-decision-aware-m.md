A decision-aware machine learning framework for essential medicines allocation was deployed across Sierra Leone as a decision support tool, addressing efficient resource distribution in low- and middle-income countries. The system incorporated multi-task learning for sample efficiency and catalytic priors to ensure equitable allocation despite limited high-quality data. An econometric evaluation found a 19% increase in consumption of allocated products in treated districts. The successful deployment was subsequently scaled nationwide, reaching an estimated 2 million women and children under five, demonstrating how machine learning can improve healthcare efficiency and access at minimal cost.
