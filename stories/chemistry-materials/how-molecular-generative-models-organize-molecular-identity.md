Generative models for molecules organize chemical identity in piecewise-constant regions separated by recurring coarse-to-fine boundaries, revealing the model's fixed internal repertoire—which structures it can produce. By explicitly tracking molecular identity through the generative process, researchers exposed this partition of producible compounds. This organization depends on the molecular representation probed, identity convention chosen, decoder stochasticity, and the metric used to compare coordinates. Across three generative architectures tested, local chemical organization stabilizes during training while the count of distinct molecular identities per neighborhood continues changing. Before treating latent spaces as chemically navigable or using them to explore chemical space systematically, this internal organization must be characterized empirically rather than assumed.
