Researchers unearthed Megacauda sungei, a Jurassic mammaliaform that swam and foraged underwater with a lifestyle resembling today's platypus. The skeleton preserved evidence of mammalian features, including the ability to swallow food and nurse its young like modern mammals. This discovery pushes the origins of water-adapted mammals and sophisticated mammalian behaviors millions of years earlier than scientists previously believed.
