Research on people who have lived beyond 110 years reveals that their immune systems continue to adapt and evolve throughout their lives. These supercentenarians show an abundance of cancer-killing immune cells, suggesting their exceptional longevity may be linked to sustained immune function. This finding contradicts typical aging patterns where immune competence declines with age. The research suggests that maintaining adaptive immunity—rather than experiencing immune decline—characterizes the healthiest, longest-lived humans. Understanding how some individuals maintain robust immune competence could reveal pathways to healthy aging and support immune function in aging populations.
