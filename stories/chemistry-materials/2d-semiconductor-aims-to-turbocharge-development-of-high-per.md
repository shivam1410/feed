Researchers built an ultrathin 2D semiconductor that conducts electricity through positively charged "holes" instead of electrons. The material is so thin it opens up completely new device designs impossible before. This could revolutionize electronics by enabling faster, more efficient devices that work in ways we couldn't build previously, transforming everything from computers to consumer gadgets.
