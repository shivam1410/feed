An artificial intelligence model trained on millions of protein measurements accurately predicts which drugs will work best for individual patients with triple-negative breast cancer. The machine learning system analyzes protein expression patterns in tissue samples and forecasts drug response, enabling personalized treatment selection. The approach bridges proteomic data and clinical outcomes. This matters because it could reduce ineffective treatments, improve survival rates, and spare patients from unnecessary side effects by matching them to drugs most likely to succeed based on their unique tumor biology before starting therapy.
