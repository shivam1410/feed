Mining operations across sub-Saharan Africa trigger large-scale deforestation of dense tropical forests, with energy transition minerals—particularly cobalt—driving especially significant forest loss. The research reveals a critical environmental paradox: sourcing minerals essential for renewable energy infrastructure requires clearing biodiverse forests. As global demand for battery minerals accelerates during the energy transition, understanding and mitigating mining-induced deforestation becomes essential. Achieving genuine climate and conservation goals simultaneously requires addressing this contradiction at the intersection of energy policy and land protection.
