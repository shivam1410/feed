Three months of semaglutide treatment—a drug used for diabetes and weight loss—slowed biological aging in female mice. The treatment improved physiological function and reduced aging hallmarks including cellular senescence and tissue degeneration. Semaglutide modulated nutrient-sensing pathways and activated genetic regulators controlling aging across species. Positive results in mice do not guarantee similar effects in humans, requiring extensive clinical trials. The findings suggest semaglutide may have anti-aging potential beyond current applications, opening research directions for repurposing existing drugs for age-related diseases.
