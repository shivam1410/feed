Large language models reveal a fundamental limit: they cannot reliably correct zero-shot errors through prompting alone. Researchers discovered that model performance depends much more on inherent familiarity with specific task definitions than on memorization of training data. This finding undercuts the effectiveness of widely-used prompting techniques for task adaptation when models lack definitional understanding. The research points to core limitations in how current LLMs internalize conceptual knowledge, suggesting that true task adaptation requires more than prompt engineering.
