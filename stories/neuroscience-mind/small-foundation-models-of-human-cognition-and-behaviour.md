Small language models fine-tuned on behavioral data match large baseline models on in-distribution tasks while demonstrating superior out-of-distribution generalization capability. Testing across diverse behavioral prediction domains, small models achieved comparable in-distribution performance to large models without incurring significant computational overhead. Critically, performance depends primarily on task stimuli and feedback signals rather than choice history alone—suggesting that efficient architectures may better capture essential task structure. This finding indicates that behavioral modeling may not fundamentally require massive parameter counts, enabling practical deployment of capable models on resource-limited devices. The results support using parameter-efficient architectures for behavioral tasks, reducing infrastructure demands and costs while maintaining prediction accuracy across diverse real-world behavioral scenarios.
