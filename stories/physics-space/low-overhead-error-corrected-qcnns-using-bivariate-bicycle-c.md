arXiv:2607.05724v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Quantum convolutional neural networks (QCNNs) combine the power of quantum computing and classical CNN for computational speedup in classification tasks. However, noise levels on state-of-the-art quantum devices remain too high for practical QCNN execution. In addition, despite the reliable surface code providing a method for error rates below a threshold value, they have a prohibitively large qubit cost. Recently introduced bivariate bicycle (BB) codes are of particular interest for their high error threshold, constant encoding rate, and linear code distance. Through simulation with realistic hardware noise sources, we demonstrate that a 4-qubit unprotected QCNN fails to converge and exhibits a worse learning rate compared to numerical simulations. Addressing both limitations, we propose a distance-4 BB quantum error-correction (QEC) technique for QCNNs. In doing so, we validate that our low-overhead QEC technique for QCNNS represents a step toward practical QCNNs.
