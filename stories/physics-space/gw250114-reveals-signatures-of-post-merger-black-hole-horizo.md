The gravitational wave detection from merger event GW250114 captured a direct wave after two black holes collided, marking the first observational evidence of signatures associated with the remnant black hole's event horizon itself. This breakthrough creates a new observational channel for directly measuring frame-dragging effects—the spinning black hole's warping of spacetime—and quantifying horizon surface gravity. Previously, black hole properties could only be inferred indirectly from orbital dynamics. Now, direct horizon observations unlock tests of general relativity in extreme gravity regimes and refinements to black hole physics. This detection represents a fundamental shift in gravitational-wave astronomy.
