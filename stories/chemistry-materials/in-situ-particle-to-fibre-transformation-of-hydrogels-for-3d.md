A three-dimensional printing method transforms hydrogel particles into aligned microfibers during extrusion, creating structural anisotropy in the final material. This directional organization of fibers significantly accelerates muscle tissue regeneration compared to conventional isotropic hydrogels. The approach represents a significant advance in biomaterial fabrication, enabling programmable structural properties that better mimic natural tissue architecture. The method advances tissue engineering and regenerative medicine by providing materials with tailored mechanical and biological properties that support tissue development.
