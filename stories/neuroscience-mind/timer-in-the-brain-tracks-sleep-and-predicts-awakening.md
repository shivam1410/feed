Chemical modifications attached to proteins in the brain could serve as an objective biomarker for sleep deprivation, according to recent mouse studies. Researchers identified specific chemical tags decorating brain proteins that correlate strongly with the degree and duration of sleep loss. This biomarker approach could enable objective quantification of sleep deprivation severity, replacing reliance on subjective reporting and behavioral observations. Such biological markers would significantly improve clinical diagnosis of sleep disorders and help researchers evaluate sleep-related interventions far more precisely than current subjective assessment methods permit.
