Global agricultural demand has increased over the past few decades, driving land-use intensification in some of the world’s most biodiverse regions. An assessment of some 40,000 species, from soil microorganisms to vertebrates, in Sumatra, Indonesia, reveals that agricultural intensification threatens biodiversity through species loss and changes in community composition across different spatial scales.
