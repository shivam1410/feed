Simulations of a fly's wiring map point to a three-neuron circuit that sets the rhythm of leg movement during walking. The model was built from connectomes, which are detailed maps of which neurons connect to which. The finding comes from computer simulations, so it is a strong lead rather than a proven mechanism. It matters because it shows how a few cells might generate the steady beat that walking needs.
