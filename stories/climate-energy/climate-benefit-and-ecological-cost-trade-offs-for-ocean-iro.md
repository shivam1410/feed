A sophisticated global biogeochemical model quantifies trade-offs between carbon dioxide removal efficiency and ecosystem impacts when applying iron fertilization to ocean biomes. Iron fertilization stimulates phytoplankton growth, absorbing atmospheric CO2, but alters marine ecosystems. Analysis across ten distinct ocean biomes reveals that climate benefits from CO2 removal differ substantially from ecological consequences. Some regions sequester more carbon but disrupt ecosystems more severely, while others show balanced outcomes. Understanding these trade-offs guides evidence-based climate policy by quantifying environmental costs alongside potential climate benefits.
