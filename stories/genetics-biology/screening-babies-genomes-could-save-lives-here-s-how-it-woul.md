Genomic screening of newborns could enable early disease detection and intervention, potentially saving lives. Multiple large-scale studies are currently underway across the globe to explore how whole-genome sequencing might be implemented at birth. The approach promises to identify genetic conditions before symptoms emerge, allowing preventive or early treatment. However, significant questions remain unresolved about practical implementation: can screening be scaled to reach all newborns? Is it technically and financially feasible for healthcare systems? Most critically, does screening actually improve health outcomes enough to justify the costs and ethical considerations?
