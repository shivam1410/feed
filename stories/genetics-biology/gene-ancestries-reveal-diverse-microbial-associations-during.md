Phylogenomic reconstruction of the last eukaryotic common ancestor's complete protein repertoire reveals that horizontal gene transfer from diverse bacteria and viruses played a crucial, foundational role in eukaryotic origins. Rather than descending from a single ancestral lineage, early eukaryotes acquired genes from multiple distinct microbial sources. This genetic archaeology reshapes understanding of eukaryotic emergence, showing that cellular complexity arose through horizontal acquisition of diverse genetic elements.
