The discovery of a Jupiter-mass object orbiting a distant star has reignited scientific debate about what qualifies as a moon versus a planet. This finding suggests exosatellites—planets orbiting other planets rather than stars—may be common enough to detect systematically, expanding our understanding of planetary system diversity. Additionally, researchers documented a rare transmissible cancer in catfish populations, expanding knowledge of contagious cancers outside mammals. These findings represent advances in both exoplanet detection methodology and understanding of disease transmission across diverse species, with implications for planetary system characterization and wildlife disease management.
