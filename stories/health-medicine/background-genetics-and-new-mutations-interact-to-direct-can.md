Controlled tumor evolution experiments in mice revealed how background genetics and acquired mutations interact to determine cancer progression. Researchers showed that an individual's genetic ancestry influences genome stability and shapes which mutations provide selective advantages, fundamentally altering cancer's evolutionary trajectory. This demonstrates genetic background actively directs tumor evolution, offering explanations for variation in cancer risk and prognosis across populations and suggesting ancestry should inform cancer risk assessment.
