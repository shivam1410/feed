Ancient whale graveyards discovered on the deep seafloor host thriving ecosystems of specialized organisms. Submarine dives in the southeastern Indian Ocean revealed an enormous accumulation of whale remains dating back 5.3 million years, where communities of bacteria, crustaceans, and other creatures depend entirely on organic material from decomposing whale bodies. These deep-sea necropolis sites show how marine megafauna death events concentrate nutrients and energy sources sustaining complex food webs in the abyssal ocean. The fossil record illuminates whale evolutionary history and demonstrates whale mortality's long-term importance in shaping deep-ocean biodiversity patterns.
