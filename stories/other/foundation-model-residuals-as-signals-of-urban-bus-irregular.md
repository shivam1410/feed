Pretrained trajectory models encode mobility patterns learned across cities, but their errors when transferred to a new city are usually treated only as performance limitations. We instead ask whether these residuals reveal systematic local conditions. Architectures such as UniTraj and TrajFM learn transferable trajectory representations, although cross-context transfer remains challenging. Using the NetMob26 bus location, ticketing, weather, network, and socioeconomic data for Niteroi, we analyze spatial and travel-time residuals of these recent trajectory foundation models. We test whether high residuals concentrate spatially, whether spatial and travel-time residuals overlap, and which urban-context variables explain their distribution. We interpret high residuals as candidate proxy signals of transit irregularity rather than direct evidence of service disruption, and analyze their contextual associations using Explainable AI (XAI). Insights into such varied environments are increasingly valuable for evaluating new urban paradigms; for instance, the 15-minute-city perspective serves as a key motivation for our approach, guiding our methods and expanding the analysis beyond standard mobility metrics.
