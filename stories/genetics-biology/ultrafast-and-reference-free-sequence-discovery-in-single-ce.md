Malva, a new computational tool, performs ultrafast, reference-free searching of raw single-cell sequences across millions of cells without requiring comparison to reference genomes or pre-existing databases. Instead, it identifies sequence variants and their functional roles directly from experimental data. This breakthrough transforms transcriptomic atlases from static archived resources into dynamic, interactive platforms for discovering gene function. Researchers can now interrogate massive single-cell datasets to understand how genes operate in specific cell types without pre-existing knowledge of those sequences or their functions, directly accelerating biological discovery.
