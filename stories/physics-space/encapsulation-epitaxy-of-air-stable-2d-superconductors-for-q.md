Scientists developed "encapsulation epitaxy," a technique to grow air-stable, two-dimensional superconducting films at 2D-3D substrate interfaces. These films remain superconducting in normal air, eliminating protective inert atmosphere requirements. The advance simplifies superconducting quantum circuit fabrication, reducing operational complexity and material costs while preserving superconducting properties for practical quantum computing applications.
