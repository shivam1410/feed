Embryonic organizer cells can direct body development across different animal types, revealing a fundamental mechanism conserved throughout the animal kingdom. These cellular organizers function similarly across diverse species, suggesting a universal principle underlying animal development. Understanding how organizer cells work illuminates evolutionary developmental biology and how genetic instructions are interpreted during embryogenesis. This discovery provides insights into how complex multicellular animals with specialized tissues and organs first emerged from simpler ancestors hundreds of millions of years ago. Core developmental mechanisms are shared across distantly related animal groups.
