A man with motor neuron disease has used an at-home brain implant for nearly two years to communicate and control his computer, reclaiming independence and quality of life. Motor neuron disease typically causes progressive paralysis, eventually preventing speech and voluntary movement. The implant translates neural signals into digital commands, bypassing damaged motor pathways. This milestone demonstrates the therapeutic potential of implantable brain-computer interfaces for severely disabled patients, offering practical hope for those facing similar neurological conditions. The durability and functionality achieved over nearly two years suggests such devices could provide sustained benefit.
