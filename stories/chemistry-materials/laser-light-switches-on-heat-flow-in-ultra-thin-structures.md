Researchers demonstrated that laser light can create substantial temperature differences exceeding 100 kelvin between adjacent layers of stacked two-dimensional materials. This light-driven thermal control represents a novel mechanism for managing heat flow in ultra-thin structures without mechanical pumps or complex external refrigeration. The approach leverages optical properties of 2D materials to generate controllable temperature gradients. This capability opens possibilities for innovative thermoelectric devices, thermal management of nanoscale electronic components, and temperature control in quantum systems requiring precise conditions.
