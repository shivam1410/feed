Whole-brain, cellular-resolution imaging in zebrafish reveals a hierarchical network connecting the thalamus and brainstem that encodes recent behavioral history and shapes future decisions, biasing animals to repeat recent choices. This network functions as an 'attractor'—a pattern pulling the system back toward previously established behavioral states. The imaging captured neural activity at single-cell resolution across the entire brain. This matters because it reveals fundamental neural mechanisms by which past experiences become encoded in brain circuits and subsequently guide decision-making, with implications for understanding decision biases in both animals and humans.
