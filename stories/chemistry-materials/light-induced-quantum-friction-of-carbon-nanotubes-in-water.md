Scientists demonstrated that near-infrared fluorescent carbon nanotubes experience light-induced quantum friction when suspended in water. Exciton interactions, excited electron states created by light absorption, slow the nanotubes' Brownian motion. This effect allows optical control of diffusion and fluid dynamics at the nanoscale. The mechanism couples photonic excitation with mechanical motion of particles. This bridges quantum optics and nanoscale mechanics in a novel way. Potential applications include optically controlled drug delivery and programmable nanofluidic devices. The work demonstrates how light-matter interactions manipulate nanoscale transport.
