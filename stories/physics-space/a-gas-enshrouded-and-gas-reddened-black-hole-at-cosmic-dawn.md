Observations of a cosmic-dawn black hole source reveal an unusual supermassive black hole in a gas-enshrouded, reddened state. The findings illuminate how early supermassive black holes may have achieved rapid mass growth during the universe's first billion years by accreting material while surrounded by gas and dust. These observations may explain the population of distant little red dots—enigmatic galaxies harboring rapidly accreting supermassive black holes. The work suggests that a heavily enshrouded phase may be a common growth mode for early universe black holes, resolving questions about their surprisingly rapid assembly.
