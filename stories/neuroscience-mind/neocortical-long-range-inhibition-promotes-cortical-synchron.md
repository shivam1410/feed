Sleep-active long-range inhibitory neurons in the mouse neocortex orchestrate widespread cortical synchronization and regulate sleep. These sparse neuronal populations distribute inhibitory signals across the cortex, coordinating neural firing patterns characteristic of sleep states. This mechanism reveals how the brain actively promotes sleep through synchronized activity rather than passively shutting down. Understanding this cortical synchronization circuit advances knowledge of sleep physiology and may inform treatments for sleep disorders and neurological conditions where sleep-wake regulation is disrupted.
