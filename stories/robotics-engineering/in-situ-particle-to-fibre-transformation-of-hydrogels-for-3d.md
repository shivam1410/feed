A novel three-dimensional printing method transforms hydrogel particles into precisely aligned microfibers during the extrusion process itself, creating structural directionality. This particle-to-fiber transformation generates anisotropic tissue architecture—material with different properties along different axes. The aligned fiber structure accelerates muscle tissue regeneration by providing biological scaffolding that guides cell growth along the fiber direction. This in situ transformation during printing simplifies manufacturing and produces more physiologically accurate tissue constructs than traditional 3D printing methods.
