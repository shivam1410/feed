A new mass spectrometry method detected ubiquitin—a small regulatory protein—attached to non-protein biomolecules, specifically glycogen. In fasting mice, glycogen accumulates substantial ubiquitination: approximately 1% of the liver's total ubiquitin pool attaches to glycogen when glycogen breaks down for energy. This expands understanding of ubiquitin's regulatory roles far beyond proteins. Ubiquitin serves multiple functions: marking proteins for degradation, signaling cell processes, and now apparently regulating metabolism through non-protein targets. The discovery suggests ubiquitination may broadly regulate energy storage and mobilization, opening new research directions into ubiquitin's metabolic roles.
