Alternate RNA decoding—a relatively understudied cellular process—generates peptide sequence modifications that produce surprising functional consequences for protein behavior. When cells read the same messenger RNA in non-standard ways, they produce proteins with chemically different sequences, altering stability, localization, and activity. Research shows these variants significantly influence which proteins accumulate in specific tissues and can drive or suppress disease states. This mechanism expands protein diversity beyond what DNA sequences alone predict, revealing a hidden layer of biological regulation. Understanding alternate decoding could explain disease mechanisms previously attributed solely to genetic mutations, open new therapeutic targets, and refine protein-function understanding.
