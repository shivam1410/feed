Recent advances in materials science are enabling passive building cooling strategies that effectively dissipate heat without reliance on energy-intensive air conditioning systems, even as global temperatures continue rising. Novel materials—potentially including advanced phase-change materials, radiative cooling surfaces, or thermal management coatings—can dissipate heat more effectively than conventional construction approaches. These innovative materials address the substantial energy consumption and significant carbon emissions associated with air conditioning systems, which remain major contributors to urban heat island effects and global climate change. Passive cooling materials could substantially reduce building dependence on electrical cooling systems during increasingly frequent heat waves and dramatically improve sustainability in hot climates worldwide. The development reflects growing scientific urgency to decouple thermal comfort and indoor climate control from energy-intensive cooling infrastructure and fossil-fuel consumption.
