Research reveals agent skills work primarily through procedural anchoring—stabilizing execution via structured steps—rather than injecting missing knowledge. Skills effectively guide agents toward reliable execution but face limitations from retrieval bottlenecks and brittle assumptions that break under unexpected conditions. Understanding this mechanism explains both skill-based agents' success on familiar tasks and their struggles when conditions deviate from training assumptions. This clarifies that skills function as reliable execution templates whose effectiveness depends on proper application context.
