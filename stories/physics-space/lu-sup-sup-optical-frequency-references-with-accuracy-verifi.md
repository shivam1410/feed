Scientists achieved unprecedented precision in timekeeping by developing two independent 176-Lu single-ion optical clocks with fractional frequency uncertainties near 10-19. The two independently built clocks agreed with each other at a tolerance of 5.7 × 10-19, confirming reproducibility. These represent the most accurate time references ever created, equivalent to losing or gaining one second over roughly 30 billion years. The achievement advances fundamental science by enabling tests of physical constants, detection of gravitational effects on time, and applications in navigation and physics research. Such precision timekeeping opens new frontiers in metrology and experimental physics. The breakthrough validates the feasibility of quantum-based timekeeping systems that could revolutionize precision measurement.
