Chiral perovskite superlattices—engineered layered materials—exhibit circularly polarized superfluorescence at room temperature, a remarkable optical property. The emission intensity and polarization of light from these materials can be controlled with weak magnetic fields, demonstrating precise optical tunability. Superfluorescence represents a collective quantum phenomenon where many particles emit light coherently and simultaneously. This room-temperature demonstration is significant because quantum phenomena typically require extreme cooling, opening possibilities for optical devices and quantum applications.
