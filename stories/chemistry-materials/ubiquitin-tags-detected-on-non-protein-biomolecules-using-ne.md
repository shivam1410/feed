Researchers developed a new mass spectrometry method to detect ubiquitin, a regulatory protein, on non-protein biomolecules for the first time. In fasting mouse livers, glycogen—the energy storage molecule—contained ubiquitin tags representing 1% of the organ's total ubiquitin during breakdown. This discovery suggests ubiquitin regulates metabolism and energy mobilization beyond its known role in protein degradation, fundamentally expanding understanding of cellular energy control mechanisms and opening new research directions in metabolic regulation.
