Researchers have determined that multidecadal fluctuations in Earth's length of day—variations spanning decades in how fast our planet rotates—are driven by gravitational torque acting on the planet. These rotational variations are resisted by electromagnetic and topographic forces operating within Earth's interior. By studying these competing forces, scientists gained new insights into the material properties and dynamics of Earth's deep interior, particularly the physical and chemical behavior of layers beneath the crust. The findings advance understanding of how different components of Earth's interior interact to influence planetary rotation. These variations, though small, carry significant implications for refined timekeeping systems and for comprehending the complex dynamics that govern our planet's fundamental behavior.
