Whole-genome duplication in early vertebrate evolution generated duplicated genes that diversified into specialized cell types. Analysis of single-cell transcriptomes from human, mouse, lizard, lamprey, and amphioxus brains shows these duplicated genes (ohnologues) were pivotal in creating neuronal and glial cell diversity distinguishing vertebrate brains. Invertebrates lack this diversity because they never underwent whole-genome duplication. The expanded gene families enabled specialization of different brain cell types, each with distinct functions and gene expression patterns. This mechanism explains how genome duplication drove cellular complexity and sophistication in vertebrate nervous systems.
