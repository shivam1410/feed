A sparse population of sleep-active long-range inhibitory neurons in the mouse neocortex orchestrate widespread cortical synchronization and promote sleep. These specialized neurons fire during sleep and send inhibitory signals across large brain regions, creating synchronized neural activity patterns characteristic of sleep states. The discovery reveals a specific cortical mechanism for sleep regulation. This matters because understanding these sleep-promoting neurons could inform treatment approaches for sleep disorders and insomnia, while demonstrating how distributed neural circuits coordinate global brain states essential for health and cognition.
