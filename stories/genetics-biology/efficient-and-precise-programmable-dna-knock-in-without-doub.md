KNIT (kilobase-scale nickase-targeting) genome editing enables highly precise DNA insertion by generating single DNA nicks rather than the double-strand breaks required by conventional genome-editing technologies. This innovative method successfully inserts DNA fragments exceeding ten kilobases in length across diverse genomic loci and multiple cell types while maintaining high editing efficiency and producing minimal unwanted off-target effects. Single-nick-based DNA insertion substantially reduces cellular toxicity and unintended genetic mutations commonly associated with double-strand break approaches—major limiting factors in therapeutic gene therapy development. KNIT's demonstrated capability for large-scale DNA insertions creates new possibilities for treating genetic diseases requiring substantial DNA additions, such as inserting functional therapeutic gene copies. This substantial technical advance represents major progress toward developing safer and more efficient therapeutic genome-editing approaches.
