Ninety percent of biomedical papers published in December and archived in PubMed show signs of AI assistance—significantly higher than previous large-language-model use estimates. This rapid uptake suggests AI tools have become integral to research workflows, from drafting manuscripts to analyzing data. The prevalence raises questions about quality control, reproducibility, and transparent reporting of AI use in scientific literature. Understanding this trend is crucial for journals, institutions, and the scientific community to establish guidelines ensuring research integrity amid accelerating AI adoption.
