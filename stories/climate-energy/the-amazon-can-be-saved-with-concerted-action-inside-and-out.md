Recent climate modeling shows the Amazon rainforest can survive projected global warming if deforestation stops. The forest maintains stability below critical tipping points as long as tree-cutting ceases entirely. This evidence indicates Amazon collapse is not inevitable, and reversing deforestation through coordinated international effort remains feasible. The finding underscores that preserving this critical carbon sink depends on concrete action, not natural inevitability.
