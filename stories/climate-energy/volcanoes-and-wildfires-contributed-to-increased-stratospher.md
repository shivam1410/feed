Satellite observations and climate model simulations show that moderate volcanic eruptions and extreme wildfires have increased water vapor in Earth's stratosphere since 2005. Aerosols from these events carry water upward and alter atmospheric circulation patterns, raising stratospheric humidity. These natural events explain roughly one-third of the observed increase in stratospheric water vapor—a contribution comparable to warming from greenhouse gases. Stratospheric water vapor affects Earth's radiation balance and ozone chemistry. This finding highlights how episodic natural phenomena significantly influence atmospheric composition on decadal timescales.
