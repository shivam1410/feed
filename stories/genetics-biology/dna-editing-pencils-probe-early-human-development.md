Adenine base editing offers a precise genome-rewriting method to investigate gene function in early human embryos without producing permanent changes. The technique modifies individual DNA bases without cutting double strands, enabling researchers to selectively turn genes on or off. The approach allows systematic probing of gene roles during developmental stages. This matters because it provides a safe, reversible tool for understanding which genes drive normal early human development, potentially revealing disease genes and developmental mechanisms while raising important questions about responsible use of genome editing in human research.
