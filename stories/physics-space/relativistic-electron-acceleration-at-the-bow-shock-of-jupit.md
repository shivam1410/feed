NASA's Juno spacecraft observed relativistic electron acceleration occurring at Jupiter's bow shock—the boundary where the solar wind collides violently with Jupiter's magnetosphere. These observations established a universal scaling law that links the size of a cosmic shock to the maximum energy of cosmic rays it can produce. The finding demonstrates that particle acceleration mechanisms at shocks follow consistent, predictable physical principles across vastly different scales and locations in space. Why it matters: Understanding how cosmic rays are accelerated to extreme relativistic energies is fundamental to astrophysics and planetary science, with practical implications for spacecraft protection and explaining high-energy phenomena observed throughout the universe.
