The brain's immune cells, called microglia, were long believed to persist unchanged throughout life. Cell lineage studies now show these cells are actually replaced by circulating immune cells from the bloodstream during aging. This previously unappreciated brain-body interaction reveals how systemic changes impact brain immunity. The discovery challenges assumptions about brain cell permanence and suggests aging involves deeper interactions between the brain and the rest of the body than previously understood, potentially reshaping research into neurodegenerative diseases.
