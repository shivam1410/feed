Chiral perovskite superlattices—layered nanostructures of specially designed perovskite materials—produce circularly polarized superfluorescence at room temperature. Superfluorescence is rare and typically requires extreme conditions; achieving it at room temperature is significant. The emission's intensity and polarization can be controlled using weak magnetic fields, enabling dynamic tuning without extreme experimental requirements. This controllability is valuable for quantum technologies and photonic devices dependent on polarized light states. The discovery opens pathways for room-temperature quantum light sources, optical sensors sensitive to magnetic fields, and spintronic devices exploiting electron spin properties, advancing practical quantum technologies.
