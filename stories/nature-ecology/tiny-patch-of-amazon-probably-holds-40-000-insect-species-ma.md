A massive biodiversity cataloguing project spanning hundreds of sites in a single Amazon rainforest patch identified approximately 40,000 insect species, many completely new to science. Researchers systematically sampled insects across all vertical layers from forest floor to canopy, capturing full ecological diversity. This reveals staggering species richness in tiny forest fragments and highlights vast undiscovered biodiversity. The comprehensive inventory provides crucial baseline data for conservation and demonstrates that tropical rainforests contain far more species than previously documented.
