Nine-residue peptides can self-assemble into hexagonal pores that stack into expandable multichannel nanofibers with defined topology. The amino acid sequence encodes specific interaction motifs controlling which structures form. The resulting nanofibrils can tile hierarchically into larger ordered assemblies. This matters because engineered peptide nanostructures could become programmable building blocks for nanotechnology applications, drug delivery systems, or tissue engineering.
