Analysis of misdemeanor bail hearings in Harris County reveals magistrate judges largely follow consistent, formula-like decision patterns. Machine learning models trained per judge achieved good fits, showing that small interpretable rules capture decisions based on factors like criminal history, age, and charge type. However, judges differ substantially in some cases, suggesting individualized information beyond court data influences outcomes. Results indicate judicial behavior approximates algorithmic decision-making more than commonly assumed. This matters because it suggests judicial decisions may be improvable through standards and transparency, yet highlights that complete algorithmic prediction may miss important individualized factors.
