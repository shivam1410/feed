The Amazon rainforest may be closer to a critical tipping point than previously thought. A potential super El Niño event combined with increased drought and fire could push the world's largest rainforest past a threshold into irreversible decline. If crossed, the ecosystem could transform fundamentally—potentially shifting from forest to savanna—with catastrophic consequences for global climate and carbon cycles. Scientists fear cumulative climate stresses could reach a point where the rainforest loses its ability to sustain itself.
