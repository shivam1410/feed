Researchers demonstrate that encrypted reasoning traces from frontier models like o1 can be decoded and exploited for distillation and data theft. Building on side-channel work using latency measures, the new technique directly decodes encrypted reasoning blocks across models and sessions. Preliminary analysis of ~7,000 public Claude Code/Codex sessions identified 62 API keys, 33 email addresses, and 33 passwords appearing exclusively within reasoning blocks. Findings reveal alignment risks—hidden reasoning can obscure reasoning, hide answers, or enable undetected cheating. This matters because cryptographic obscuring of reasoning may provide limited security against determined attackers.
