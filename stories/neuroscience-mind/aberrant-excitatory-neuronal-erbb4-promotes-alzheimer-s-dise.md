Research challenges a foundational assumption in Alzheimer's disease research: that neuroinflammation—the brain's immune response—is central to early cognitive decline. Instead, findings suggest aberrant excitatory signaling through the ERBB4 receptor on neurons may drive initial pathology, with inflammation playing a secondary or unnecessary role in early synapse loss. This distinction matters significantly for drug development, as current Alzheimer's therapies predominantly target inflammatory pathways. If neuroinflammation proves dispensable for early disease, resources may be better invested in understanding excitatory signaling dysregulation and developing ERBB4-targeting interventions.
