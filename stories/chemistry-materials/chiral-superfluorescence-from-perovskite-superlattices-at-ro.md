Chiral perovskite superlattices—engineered quantum materials with layered structures—achieve circularly polarized superfluorescence at room temperature, a quantum phenomenon where coherent light emission occurs with controlled circular polarization. The intensity and polarization state of the emitted light can be precisely tuned and modulated using weak external magnetic fields, enabling dynamic control. This represents a significant advance in room-temperature quantum optics. Why it matters: Achieving room-temperature quantum optical effects in perovskite materials opens new possibilities for quantum technologies, including applications in quantum computing, quantum sensing, and quantum information processing where stable, controllable quantum light sources are essential yet currently difficult to implement.
