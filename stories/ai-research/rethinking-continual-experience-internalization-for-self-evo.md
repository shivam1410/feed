Experience internalization enables large language models to learn continuously by converting past interactions into reusable capabilities without requiring complete retraining. Research examined critical design choices affecting this process: experience granularity (the appropriate level of detail for breaking down past interactions), injection patterns (various methods for integrating experiences back into models), and internalization regimes (stability mechanisms that maintain learning performance over extended interaction periods). These findings provide actionable guidance for building self-evolving agents that genuinely improve over time through accumulated experience, addressing a fundamental challenge in enabling models to grow smarter through direct interaction.
