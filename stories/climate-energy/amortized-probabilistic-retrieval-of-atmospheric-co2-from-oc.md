A novel deep learning framework retrieves atmospheric CO2 concentrations from NASA's OCO-2 satellite spectra orders of magnitude faster than traditional operational algorithms. The approach employs Laplace approximations and normalizing flows for rigorous quantification of measurement uncertainties. Trained on high-fidelity physics simulations incorporating realistic forward model errors, the method achieves superior predictive accuracy compared to baseline physics-based inversion methods. The dramatic speed improvements and principled uncertainty quantification enable real-time processing of massive satellite data streams, which is critical for constraining global carbon budgets and advancing climate science.
