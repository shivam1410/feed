Using high-resolution microtomography and phylogenetic analysis, researchers studied Urokodia aequalis, a 500-million-year-old fossil from the Chengjiang biota of China. This exceptionally preserved specimen provided crucial insights into the evolutionary origins of two defining chelicerate structures: the chelicerae (specialized feeding appendages) and book gills (respiratory organs). The fossil evidence reinterpreted how these structures evolved, illuminating early evolutionary history and developmental processes that generated the distinctive anatomy of this major arthropod group.
