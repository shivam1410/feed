They built OCELOT, a model that maps where species are likely to be found. It was trained on 2 billion wildlife records covering 100,000 taxa. Most sightings cluster near people, so the team tested how well it predicts remote, unvisited areas. Satellite foundation embeddings improved those predictions by 51% over coordinate-only models. The model also matches or beats existing baselines on standard interpolation tests. Better range maps help interpret camera trap and audio data from far-off places.
