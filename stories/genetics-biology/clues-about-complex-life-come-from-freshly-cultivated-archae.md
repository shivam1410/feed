Researchers have grown two archaeal strains in the lab. These microbes belong to a lineage closely related to the ancestor of complex cells, which have membrane-bound organelles. The strains show simple internal organization, elaborate surface structures, microbial partnerships and a protein-dependent lifestyle. Those traits offer clues about how complex cells arose.
