Research reveals that sleep and exercise can reduce the growth rate of clonal hematopoiesis, the expansion of mutant blood cells accumulating with age. The effect varies depending on the specific mutation present, indicating mutation-dependent responses to lifestyle interventions. These factors limit atherosclerosis development driven by mutant cells. Clonal hematopoiesis increases cardiovascular disease risk in aging populations. The mutation-dependent responses suggest different biological mechanisms activate for different genetic variants. This finding suggests personalized lifestyle interventions could reduce cardiovascular disease risk by slowing age-related blood cell mutations.
