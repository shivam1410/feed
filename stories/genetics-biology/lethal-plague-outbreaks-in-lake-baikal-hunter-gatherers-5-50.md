Analysis of ancient DNA from Siberian hunter-gatherers near Lake Baikal reveals the plague bacterium Yersinia pestis caused lethal outbreaks 5,500 years ago. This is far earlier than previously documented plague cases and geographically distant from Late Neolithic Europe, where disease was previously thought to have first emerged. The finding substantially revises the known timeline of plague's geographic origin and emergence, suggesting the disease originated in eastern Asia thousands of years before reaching European populations. Ancient DNA analysis enabled researchers to identify the pathogen from skeletal remains and characterize its virulence. This discovery reframes understanding of plague's natural history.
