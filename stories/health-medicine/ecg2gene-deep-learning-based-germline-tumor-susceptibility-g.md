ECG2Gene reads routine 12-lead heart traces to estimate inherited cancer risk. It first learns six gene-risk scores from ECGs of people with known variants. A second model then combines those scores with clinical data to predict future cancer. In UK Biobank, the best sex-specific models scored 0.683 for breast cancer, 0.633 for ovarian and 0.660 for prostate (AUROC, a ranking score). In two outside cohorts, adding age gave prostate cancer scores of 0.709 and 0.579. It needs no invasive tests, only routine ECGs and clinical variables.
