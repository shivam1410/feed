Modified bacteria could be engineered to manufacture and deliver anticancer drugs directly within the body, offering a revolutionary drug-delivery approach. Rather than synthesizing drugs in laboratories, genetically modified microorganisms would produce therapeutic compounds and transport them to cancer cells. This biological manufacturing and delivery system leverages bacterial genetics and metabolism for pharmaceutical purposes. The approach matters because it could reduce manufacturing costs, improve drug targeting precision, and enable localized treatment delivery with potentially fewer side effects than traditional systemic drug administration.
