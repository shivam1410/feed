Pumas provide an unexpected traffic safety benefit. Where mountain lions patrol, vehicle collisions with deer drop significantly. The mechanism is ecological: pumas prey on deer, thereby reducing their populations and altering their spatial distribution in regions where predators are active. This means fewer deer on roadways, translating to fewer accident-causing wildlife encounters. The finding has practical road safety implications, suggesting that predator presence influences prey distribution with indirect benefits for human infrastructure—a rare case where apex predators improve human safety metrics.
