A well-preserved fossil snake from Brazil's Late Cretaceous period reveals that the earliest snakes already displayed considerable diversity in brain structure and ecological niches. This finding indicates that brain morphology and ecological specialization emerged very early in snake evolution, suggesting the group's adaptive toolkit was established before the Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction. Understanding these early traits provides insight into how modern snake diversity originated from a common ancestor.
