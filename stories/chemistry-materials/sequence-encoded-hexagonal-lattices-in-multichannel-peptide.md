Nine-residue peptide sequences can encode discrete interaction motifs that self-assemble into hexagonal pores, which hierarchically organize into laterally expandable multichannel nanofibrils with defined topology. This demonstrates programmable peptide assembly where sequence directly controls nanoscale architecture. Applications include drug delivery platforms, biosensing devices, and artificial tissue engineering scaffolds.
