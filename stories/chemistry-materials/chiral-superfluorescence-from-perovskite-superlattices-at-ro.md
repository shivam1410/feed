Engineered perovskite superlattices produce circularly polarized light through superfluorescence at room temperature, with emission controllable by weak magnetic fields. This achievement eliminates the need for extreme cryogenic cooling that previously hindered quantum optical devices. The ability to control light polarization with small magnetic fields enables practical applications in quantum computing and advanced optical technologies without requiring expensive infrastructure.
