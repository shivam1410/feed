MFP uses count-based Morgan fingerprints computed per reaction component, aggregated by chemical role, and combined with difference features to predict reaction yield. On Suzuki-Miyaura reactions it achieves R²=0.878, on Buchwald-Hartwig R²=0.969, while training an order of magnitude faster than graph or Transformer methods. Complexity analysis shows all representation cost folds into one-time preprocessing, eliminating per-epoch overhead.
