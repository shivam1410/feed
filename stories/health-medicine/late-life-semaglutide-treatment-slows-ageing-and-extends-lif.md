Female mice receiving semaglutide for three months showed improved physiological function, reduced aging hallmarks, and changes in nutrient-sensing pathways and conserved genetic regulators of aging. Semaglutide is a drug originally developed to treat type 2 diabetes by mimicking glucagon-like peptide-1, a hormone regulating blood sugar and appetite. Recent research has explored whether semaglutide might slow aging processes more broadly. These mouse study results suggest the drug improves multiple measures of health and functionality while modulating ancient biological pathways controlling lifespan and aging rates. These pathways are conserved across species, suggesting findings in mice may translate to humans. The results raise the possibility that semaglutide could slow aging in mammals, though human studies would be needed to confirm whether anti-aging benefits observed in mice extend to people.
