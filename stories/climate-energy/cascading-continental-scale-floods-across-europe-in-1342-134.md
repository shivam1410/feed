A paleoclimate analysis of cascading continental-scale flood events across Europe in 1342–1343 demonstrates that unusually severe sequences of extreme floods occurred during the medieval period. The research reveals that current flood management infrastructure and risk assessment frameworks are unprepared for such multi-event cascades. The historical floods show that extreme events can compound across regions over short timeframes. These findings highlight the urgent need for flood management strategies specifically designed to handle unusual series of extreme events, not only single catastrophic events, emphasizing the importance of proactive preparation for compound disasters.
