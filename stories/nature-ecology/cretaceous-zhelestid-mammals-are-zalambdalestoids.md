New Mongolian fossil discoveries reveal that zhelestid mammals were not early placental-like relatives but a dental variant within Zalambdalestoidea. Zhelestids had distinctive molars alongside characteristic zalambdalestid skull and limb anatomy. This reclassification refines understanding of early mammal evolution during the Cretaceous period. Fossil analysis showing multiple diagnostic features—dental, cranial, and postcranial—provides robust evidence for the taxonomic reassessment. Accurately placing extinct mammals on the evolutionary tree is crucial for understanding how modern mammalian groups originated and diversified. These findings demonstrate that morphological details once considered definitive of major groups were distributed among related lineages in more complex ways.
