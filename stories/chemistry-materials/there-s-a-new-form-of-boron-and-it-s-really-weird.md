A new boron phase created under high pressure exhibits extraordinary properties: it is a million times more electrically conductive than chemists predicted and can be mechanically stretched. This anomalous behavior challenges existing understanding of boron's electronic and mechanical properties, raising questions about other extreme-condition phases and their potential applications in electronics or structural materials.
