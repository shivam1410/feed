Scientists have identified the chemical mechanism controlling naked mole-rats' unusual social hierarchy, solving a long-standing puzzle in behavioral biology. Colonies of these remarkable mammals function like insect hives with a single reproducing queen suppressing fertility in all subordinate members. The discovery reveals how these animals maintain and enforce such extreme reproductive suppression through chemical signals, representing a unique mammalian social system. Understanding this mechanism provides insights into how social dominance operates in unusual animal societies.
