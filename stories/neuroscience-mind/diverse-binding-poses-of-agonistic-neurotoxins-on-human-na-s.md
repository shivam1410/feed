Structural studies of three animal-derived neurotoxins—bullet ant toxin, cone snail toxin, and scorpion toxin—reveal multiple distinct binding poses when bound to human Nav1.6 sodium channels. Despite targeting the same ion channel, these toxins adopt different structural conformations and activate channels through different mechanistic pathways. Understanding this structural and functional diversity informs drug development strategies and reveals how evolution creates multiple independent solutions to ion channel modulation.
