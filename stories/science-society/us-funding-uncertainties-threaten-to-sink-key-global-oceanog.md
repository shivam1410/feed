The United States has historically led global ocean observation, but current funding cuts and threats to future support have oceanography researchers concerned it will no longer be a dependable partner. Ocean monitoring requires sustained, long-term infrastructure investment and expertise concentrated in US institutions. Cuts to US programs threaten to fragment international oceanographic collaborations and reduce global capacity to track changing ocean conditions. This risks undermining understanding of ocean health during climate change and ocean acidification, which require coordinated long-term monitoring.
