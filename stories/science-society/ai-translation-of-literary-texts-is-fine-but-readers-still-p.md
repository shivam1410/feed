Despite metrics indicating competitive machine translation quality, human readers consistently prefer human-translated literary works over AI translations. Readers report finding machine-translated literature less immersive, suggesting they perceive qualitative differences that standard translation metrics fail to capture. This preference emerges even when readers cannot articulate specific flaws, indicating literary translation quality involves subtle stylistic and emotional dimensions beyond lexical accuracy. The gap between machine translation metrics and human reader experience highlights a fundamental challenge: automated metrics optimize for word-level fidelity while literature demands preservation of voice, tone, and cultural resonance. Machine translation excels at technical domains but lags significantly in preserving the artistry central to literature.
