Two recent demonstrations showcase silicon-based quantum processors that address key engineering challenges for building scalable quantum computers. Silicon quantum computing offers advantages: silicon is abundant, established semiconductor manufacturing can be adapted, and integration with classical electronics is possible. The demonstrations tackle specific engineering hurdles including qubit stability, error correction, scalability, and fabrication consistency. Silicon-based approaches complement other quantum computing platforms. These advances suggest a practical path toward fault-tolerant quantum computers by leveraging existing semiconductor industry expertise and infrastructure. Solving silicon's challenges could accelerate the timeline for quantum computers to achieve practical applications.
