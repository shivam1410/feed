Ancient Amazon societies were far larger and more sophisticated than previously believed, according to aerial laser-scanning surveys. The technology revealed more than 20,000 human-made earthworks concentrated in less than three percent of the Greater Amazonia region. Evidence indicates these societies flourished between AD 100 and 300, supporting an estimated population of 1.25 million to 3 million people across the surveyed area. Laser scanning penetrated dense rainforest vegetation, detecting earthworks invisible to traditional ground surveys and completely altering understanding of pre-Columbian Amazonian settlement. The discoveries demonstrate that the Amazon was not pristine wilderness but extensively engineered landscape shaped by sophisticated civilizations. These ancient societies fundamentally altered soil properties, vegetation composition, and forest structure through earthwork construction and land management practices that remain visible today. The findings demand comprehensive reassessment of pre-Columbian Amazonian history, ecology, and human influence.
