Geochemical analysis provides evidence that worm-like organisms living during the Ediacaran period engaged in symbiotic relationships with bacteria, pushing back the known history of animal-microbe partnerships to Earth's earliest complex life forms. Such mutually beneficial relationships likely provided significant survival advantages, suggesting that cooperation between organisms and bacteria was fundamental to establishing complex life on Earth.
