The SyeA protein, present in the ancient Buchnera ancestor, is essential for bacterial endosymbiont colonization of host cells. Secreted into the host cytoplasm, it resembles effectors from pathogenic bacteria. This discovery reveals how symbiotic bacteria evolved host-invasion mechanisms, illuminating the evolutionary origin of intracellular symbiosis. Understanding SyeA mechanism clarifies how bacterial endosymbionts became permanent cellular residents.
