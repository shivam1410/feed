Researchers identified a peptide signaling mechanism that protects crop plants during cold stress—a critical vulnerability as climate volatility increases. Small RGF-family peptides trigger cold-resilience pathways in both tomatoes and rice, preventing catastrophic yield losses during temperature extremes. By understanding the molecular switches controlling this response, scientists can engineer climate-resilient crops without sacrificing yield. This demonstrates how protein structure knowledge enables climate adaptation: identifying and manipulating specific peptide-receptor interactions unlocks crop hardiness. The mechanism works in two major food crops, indicating broad agricultural potential. As extreme weather events intensify globally, crops engineered for cold tolerance become essential infrastructure for food security and climate adaptation in vulnerable regions.
