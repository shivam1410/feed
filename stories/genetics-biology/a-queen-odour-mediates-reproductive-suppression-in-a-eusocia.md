Naked mole-rat queens produce isopropyl myristate, a low-volatility chemical ester that maintains reproductive suppression in colony members. The compound is detected by subordinate animals and alters prolactin and progesterone hormones, preventing breeding while preserving the queen's reproductive monopoly. This molecular mechanism underlies the colony's eusocial structure, explaining how one pheromone maintains extreme reproductive skew. The finding provides biochemical evidence for pheromonal control of mammalian reproductive hierarchies.
