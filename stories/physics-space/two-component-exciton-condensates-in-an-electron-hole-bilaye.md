Macroscopic quantum coherence emerged in two-component exciton Bose-Einstein condensates within engineered MoSe2/hBN/WSe2 electron-hole bilayers. These novel quantum states exhibited distinct spin-valley polarized phases and underwent quantum phase transitions under applied magnetic fields. The exciton condensates remained stable at temperatures up to approximately 1.8 K. This achievement demonstrates quantum phenomena—macroscopic quantum states normally observed in superfluid helium or ultracold atoms—can occur in solid-state engineered materials. The findings validate theoretical predictions and open possibilities for quantum devices exploiting coherent exciton states with applications in quantum computing and sensing.
