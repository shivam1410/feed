Researchers discovered that Streptomyces bacteria produce a naturally evolved combination therapy: a conserved megacluster of biosynthetic genes encodes an arsenal of structurally distinct antimicrobials that all converge on a single bacterial target—biotin biosynthesis. Rather than producing one antibiotic, bacteria deploy multiple compounds against the same vulnerability, mimicking modern medical combination therapy strategies. This natural synergy approach likely evolved to overwhelm bacterial resistance mechanisms, since resistance to one compound faces competition from others. The discovery reveals combination therapies aren't modern inventions but ancient survival strategies bacteria evolved. This insight could guide antibiotic development, suggesting combination drugs might overcome resistance challenges.
