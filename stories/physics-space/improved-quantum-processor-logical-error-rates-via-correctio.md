Scientists applied quantum error-correcting codes combined with error detection and post-selection to a trapped-ion quantum processor, achieving logical error rate improvements ranging from 11-fold to 800-fold compared with baseline circuit designs. These improvements represent major progress toward fault-tolerant quantum computing, where logical qubits are more reliable than physical qubits. Trapped-ion processors manipulate individual ions with lasers. This matters because reducing logical error rates is the central challenge preventing quantum computers from scaling to practical applications. With sufficient error reduction, larger quantum computers could tackle real-world problems in drug discovery and materials science that classical computers cannot solve.
