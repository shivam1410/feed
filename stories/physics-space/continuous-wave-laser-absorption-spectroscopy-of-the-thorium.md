Continuous-wave laser absorption spectroscopy of thorium-229 nuclei in a thorium-doped calcium fluoride crystal demonstrates a new and much more efficient path toward building solid-state optical nuclear clocks with high stability and robustness. Researchers successfully studied thorium-229 nuclear excited states using this technique within crystal material. The key finding is that this approach is substantially more efficient than previous ways of attempting to build such clocks. Optical nuclear clocks require exceptional stability and robustness for precision timekeeping, qualities this crystal-based method can provide. The work establishes a promising research direction and demonstrates that solid-state optical nuclear clock construction is more achievable than previously understood. This represents meaningful progress toward practical optical nuclear clocks for timekeeping applications.
