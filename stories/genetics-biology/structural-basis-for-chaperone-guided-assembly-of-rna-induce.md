Researchers determined how cells construct the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), the molecular machinery regulating gene expression. Structural analysis of the AGO maturation complex revealed that molecular chaperones guide assembly by orchestrating interactions between proteins and RNA duplexes. High-resolution structures show precise atomic-level interactions driving this multi-step assembly. Understanding these mechanisms clarifies how cells build and deploy RISC complexes to silence genes—a fundamental process and therapeutic target for RNA interference drugs designed to silence disease-causing genes in patients.
