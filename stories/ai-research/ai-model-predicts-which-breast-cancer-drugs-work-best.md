An artificial intelligence model trained on millions of protein measurements can predict which breast cancer drugs will be most effective for individual patients. Researchers developed this tool specifically for triple-negative breast cancer, an aggressive form lacking three common hormone receptors. By analyzing protein expression patterns in tissue samples, the AI identifies drug-response signatures predictive of treatment success. This advancement addresses a critical clinical challenge: triple-negative breast cancer is particularly difficult to treat, and personalized drug prediction could spare patients from ineffective therapies while directing them toward drugs most likely to work, improving outcomes and reducing unnecessary side effects.
