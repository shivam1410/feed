European countries including Spain and France can expect fire regimes increasingly similar to California's as climate change intensifies hot, dry conditions. Scientists project that rising temperatures and reduced precipitation will fundamentally transform southern European fire dynamics, extending fire seasons and increasing wildfire frequency and intensity. This shift poses severe risks to ecosystems adapted to historical fire patterns, threatens agricultural production, and endangers communities. Adaptation will require major changes in land management, forest policy, and disaster preparedness. The findings underscore how climate change is rapidly reshaping regional hazard profiles and necessitating preventive action.
