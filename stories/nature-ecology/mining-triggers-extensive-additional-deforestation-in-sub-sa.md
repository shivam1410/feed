Mining operations across sub-Saharan Africa are driving extensive deforestation of dense tropical forests, a study finds. Energy transition minerals, particularly cobalt used in renewable energy technology and batteries, emerge as a major driver of this forest loss. The research documents the large-scale environmental costs of mining expansion in the region. This connection reveals a critical paradox: while minerals extracted for green energy transition support global climate goals, their extraction destroys carbon-absorbing forests vital for local and global climate regulation.
