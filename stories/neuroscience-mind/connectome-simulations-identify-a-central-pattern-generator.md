A tiny circuit of just three neurons may set the walking rhythm for a fly's legs. Researchers ran computer simulations of a network built from fly connectomes, which are wiring maps of the nervous system. The simulations pointed to this three-neuron circuit as one organized to produce leg motor rhythms. Those rhythms are the basic beat of walking. It matters because it names a specific, small circuit behind a basic movement.
