In a mouse model of Huntington's disease, targeted neuromodulation restored network excitation-inhibition balance and reversed motor deficits. Researchers selectively modulated neuronal activity to restore normal network connectivity and circuit function. The manipulation reversed disease hallmarks, demonstrating that circuit dysfunction in neurodegeneration can be functionally rescued through targeted intervention. This finding suggests therapeutic strategies beyond genetic correction: even with permanent genetic mutations intact, restoring network equilibrium can alleviate disease symptoms.
