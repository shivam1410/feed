Tin perovskites are promising semiconductors hampered by surface-defect instability and chemical drift. A volatile-assisted coordination strategy chemically stabilizes surfaces, preventing defect-mediated degradation and self-doping processes. Transistors built with this approach maintain stable operation for one month at 100°C, meeting critical durability benchmarks for commercial electronics. The chemical engineering overcomes a major obstacle to commercializing tin perovskites: their inherent susceptibility to surface degradation. This breakthrough could accelerate tin perovskite deployment in practical electronic applications.
