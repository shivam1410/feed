In mice, a sparse population of long-range inhibitory neurons distributed across the neocortex actively promotes sleep through widespread cortical synchronization. These sleep-active neurons extend connections across distant brain regions and coordinate synchronized activity patterns associated with sleep states. The discovery identifies a specific cortical circuit mechanism that regulates sleep, highlighting how focused neuronal populations orchestrate global brain states. Understanding these circuits advances sleep neurobiology knowledge and may inform approaches to sleep disorders.
