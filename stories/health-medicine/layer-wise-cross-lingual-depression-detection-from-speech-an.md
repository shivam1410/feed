A supervised contrastive alignment framework enables cross-lingual depression detection from speech by mapping WavLM embeddings from English and Mandarin into a shared clinical space. The system reveals speaker identity leakage: models exploited speaker characteristics rather than clinical depression markers, inflating apparent performance. The framework uses contrastive alignment to separate genuine depression signals from speaker identity noise. By learning shared representations across languages, it tackles the fundamental challenge of generalization when training and testing on different languages. This cross-lingual approach addresses a critical gap in mental health assessment, where diagnostic systems must work reliably across diverse populations while avoiding spurious correlations that look predictive but lack clinical validity.
