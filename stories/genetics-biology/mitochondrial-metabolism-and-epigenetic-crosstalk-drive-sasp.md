In senescent cells, mitochondria release acetyl-CoA, which promotes histone acetylation and increases chromatin accessibility at inflammatory gene loci, driving the senescence-associated secretory phenotype. Blocking the SLC25A1 transporter protein attenuates these effects, revealing therapeutic potential. This discovery highlights how mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic modifications are coupled in aging. The mitochondrial-epigenetic crosstalk appears central to aging-associated inflammation. Targeting this pathway could delay age-related functional decline by interrupting the inflammatory cascade that accelerates cellular aging. This reveals new therapeutic targets for age-related diseases.
