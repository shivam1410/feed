PRISM adapts subtraction mapping from human neuroscience to study language model internals, enabling direct comparison of cognitive specialization in neural networks and brains. Applied to LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-13B across seven Philadelphia Naming Test categories and compared against lesion patterns in 213 post-stroke aphasia patients, PRISM uses threshold-free cluster enhancement along the layer axis for both perturbed transformers and brain lesion-symptom correlations. Both substrates recovered a robust phonemic-favoring dissociation: deep layers and perisylvian cortex clusters, demonstrating that language models exhibit brain-like functional specialization.
