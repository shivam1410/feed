AlphaFold3 predictions of molecular contacts made by a genome-editing enzyme have improved the enzyme's selectivity for target DNA sequences. By computationally predicting the detailed intermolecular interactions within CRISPR-editing complexes, researchers can now identify and design mutations that enhance specificity. This approach uses machine learning to model how the editing enzyme engages with different DNA targets, revealing structural features that promote precise binding. CRISPR enzymes frequently show off-target activity, cutting unintended genomic sites. By using AI to understand and optimize molecular contacts at the enzyme-DNA interface, researchers can design CRISPR variants with reduced off-target effects.
