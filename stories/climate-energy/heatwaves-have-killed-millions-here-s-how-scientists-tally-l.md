Scientists use two fundamentally different methods to estimate heatwave deaths. Direct attribution counts heat as primary cause; statistical approaches compare mortality during heatwaves to baseline expectations. As severe heatwaves affect Asia and Europe, these methods often produce different totals, reflecting indirect deaths, masked causes, and regional variation. Reconciling these approaches matters for accurate public health planning and understanding the true mortality burden of heat extremes in a warming climate.
