Analysis of enamel nanocrystal misorientation—deviation from perfect crystal alignment—across primate and human teeth reveals changes corresponding to three major dietary shifts in human evolution. Misorientation increased as diets shifted toward harder foods during meat-eating adoption and agriculture. Counterintuitively, this misorientation enhances rather than weakens enamel resilience, suggesting enamel structure evolved to withstand mechanical stresses imposed by harder, more varied diets.
