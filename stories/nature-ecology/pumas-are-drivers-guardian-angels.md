Puma populations provide an unexpected safety benefit to drivers and wildlife. In regions where these apex predators actively hunt and patrol, vehicle collisions with deer occur significantly less frequently. The mechanism is ecological: puma predation reduces local deer population densities in puma territories, thereby lowering the probability of deer-vehicle encounters on roads. This finding reveals a compelling conservation co-benefit—protecting apex predators simultaneously improves driver safety, reduces wildlife mortality from vehicle strikes, and supports ecosystem function.
