A new chemical and biological redox process converts lignin from poplar wood into adipic acid, a single valuable bioproduct, achieved in high yields. The process resembles methods used in petrochemical refining, combining chemical transformation with biological catalysis. Adipic acid is an important industrial chemical widely used in manufacturing polymers. This matters because it demonstrates that lignin—a major structural component of wood often discarded as waste—can be efficiently converted into high-value chemicals using a hybrid approach, potentially enabling sustainable production from abundant renewable feedstocks.
