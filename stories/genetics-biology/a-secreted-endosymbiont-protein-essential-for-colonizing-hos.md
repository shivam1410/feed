SyeA is an ancient bacterial protein essential for symbiotic colonization of host cells. Originally present in the Buchnera ancestor, this protein is secreted into the host cytoplasm and structurally resembles secretion effectors deployed by bacterial pathogens. Research confirms it is essential for transmitting Buchnera to new host cells. This discovery reveals how obligate symbionts evolved mechanisms originally honed by pathogenic bacteria to establish and maintain host relationships. Understanding these molecular foundations of endosymbiosis illuminates how bacteria-host partnerships transformed from transient infections into permanent cellular residents.
