Psilocybin reorganizes brain activity into highly structured patterns that align with contextual information from both internal and external sources. This neural reorganization creates an integrated brain state where the boundaries between self-awareness and external environment become fluid. The study reveals the neural mechanism underlying reported psychological effects of psychedelics—the felt sense of continuity and connection with surroundings. Understanding this reorganization explains how psychedelics may facilitate psychological change and supports research into their therapeutic potential for mental health conditions. The structured, context-aligned brain activity provides a neural basis for transformation of consciousness.
