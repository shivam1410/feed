Existing theories about how ice crystals grow in cooling liquids are wildly inaccurate when compared with experimental data. Scientists are now illuminating the earliest moments of the freezing process, filling critical gaps in fundamental physics understanding. These insights matter broadly: accurate ice-formation models are essential for predicting cloud behavior in atmospheric systems, understanding materials science, and designing industrial applications. The findings challenge decades of theoretical assumptions about phase transitions.
