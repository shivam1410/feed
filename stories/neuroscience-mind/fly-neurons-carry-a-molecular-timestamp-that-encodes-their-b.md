A comprehensive single-cell gene-expression atlas of the Drosophila nerve cord, integrated with detailed circuit connectivity mapping data, has uncovered a remarkably simple shared molecular code that enables neurons to precisely encode their developmental birth order from their parent cell of origin. Each neuron carries a distinctive transcriptional 'timestamp' that reflects when it was generated during embryonic neural development, information that profoundly shapes the neuron's subsequent cellular identity and functional diversity. This molecular birth-order code operates consistently across different neural lineages and neuronal types, suggesting a fundamental and conserved principle governing neural development. The molecular birth-order record influences how individual neurons integrate into functional circuits and develop their characteristic properties and connectivity patterns. This discovery reveals how development encodes temporal information directly into neuronal identity, with broad implications for understanding neural complexity across diverse organisms.
