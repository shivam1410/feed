Electric vehicles are cutting pollution-related premature deaths in China, preventing approximately 260,000 deaths. However, their impact is selective—while they significantly reduce some air pollutants, others remain largely unaffected by vehicle electrification. This represents a substantial public health achievement from addressing vehicle emissions in one of the world's largest pollution sources. Yet the findings highlight limitations: comprehensive air quality improvements require simultaneously addressing multiple pollution sources and types beyond vehicles. The research indicates that electrification is an important but incomplete solution to China's persistent air quality challenges, requiring complementary strategies to tackle remaining pollutants.
