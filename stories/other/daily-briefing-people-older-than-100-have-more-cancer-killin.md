People over age 100 possess elevated levels of killer T cells—immune cells that attack cancer—suggesting immune function contributes to exceptional longevity. Researchers also developed the longest-lived human brain organoids to date, advancing laboratory modeling of neural development and disease. Additionally, mass genome-screening programs for newborns are emerging as preventive medicine tools. These developments collectively advance understanding of aging mechanisms, regenerative medicine, and early disease detection.
