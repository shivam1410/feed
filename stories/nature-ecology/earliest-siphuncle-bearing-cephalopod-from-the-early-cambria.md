Early Cambrian fossil Eoceras shaanxiense preserves septa and a segmented tube resembling a primordial siphuncle, the chambered structure used for buoyancy in modern cephalopods. This discovery extends cephalopod evolutionary origins back further than previously known and reveals how early buoyancy regulation mechanisms originated. The finding illuminates the assembly of shell architecture in the ancestors of nautiluses, squid, and octopuses.
