Artificial intelligence systems trained on medical records can inadvertently expose patient identities through analysis of their training data, a privacy vulnerability with particularly severe consequences for underrepresented groups. Machine-learning models can be reverse-engineered to extract identifying information about individuals in their training datasets. Underrepresented populations face heightened identification risk because their data is relatively sparse in training sets, making individual records more statistically distinctive and easier to isolate. This finding matters for medical privacy and AI ethics because it reveals a serious vulnerability in how health information is used for AI development, particularly affecting vulnerable populations.
