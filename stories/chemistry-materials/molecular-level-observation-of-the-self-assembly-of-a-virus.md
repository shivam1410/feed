Using mass photometry combined with single-molecule trapping, researchers achieve real-time molecular-resolution observation of individual virus-like particle assembly. Mass photometry measures extremely small mass changes while single-molecule trapping keeps particles in observation range. The technology enables direct monitoring of how protein subunits self-assemble into complete viral structures with molecular detail. This reveals assembly pathways—the precise sequence of steps and intermediates formed during particle construction—and their dynamics. Previously scientists could only infer assembly processes indirectly. Direct observation of assembly mechanisms provides insights into viral structure formation applicable to vaccine and nanotechnology design. The method represents a major advance in understanding biological self-assembly at molecular resolution.
