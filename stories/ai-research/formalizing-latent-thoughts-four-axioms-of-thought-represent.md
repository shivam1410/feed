This research introduces an axiomatic framework for evaluating how language models internally represent thoughts and reasoning processes. Testing multiple LLM architectures across various reasoning tasks, the framework reveals that current models systematically fail to satisfy fundamental mathematical axioms—principles that should theoretically underpin meaningful thought representation. These failures appear consistently across different model sizes and architectures, suggesting a fundamental limitation rather than an implementation detail. This matters because it challenges widespread assumptions that large language models genuinely reason internally; instead, they may excel at pattern-matching without implementing true reasoning. Understanding these gaps is crucial for developing more reliable reasoning systems and for honestly assessing AI capabilities in high-stakes applications requiring genuine logical reasoning.
