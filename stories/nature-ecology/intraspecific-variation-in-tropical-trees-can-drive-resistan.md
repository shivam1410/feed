Different tropical tree species—and distinct populations within the same species—vary in their ability to withstand drought, suggesting local adaptation to regional water availability. Understanding drought tolerance variation is critical for predicting forest resilience as climate change alters precipitation and increases drought frequency. Tropical forests play major roles in global carbon cycling and biodiversity. Identifying which populations or species are more drought-tolerant informs forest management and conservation strategies. This work highlights how evolutionary adaptation within species influences ecosystem stability under environmental change.
