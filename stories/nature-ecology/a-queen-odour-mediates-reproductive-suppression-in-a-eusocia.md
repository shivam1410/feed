Naked mole-rat queens produce isopropyl myristate, a low-volatility chemical ester that high-ranking colony members detect and actively avoid. Detection of this compound triggers hormonal changes in other females, specifically altering prolactin and progesterone levels to suppress reproduction and preserve the colony's eusocial hierarchy. This discovery reveals how a single pheromone maintains one of nature's most extreme and unusual mammalian social systems, where reproductive roles are completely determined by chemical signaling. The finding demonstrates the remarkable power of chemical communication.
