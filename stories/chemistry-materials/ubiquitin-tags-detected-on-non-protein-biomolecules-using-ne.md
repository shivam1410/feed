A new mass spectrometry method detected ubiquitin protein tags on non-protein biomolecules, revealing unexpected regulatory mechanisms. In mouse liver, glycogen (an energy-storing molecule) carries ubiquitin tags during fasting, accounting for 1% of the liver's total ubiquitin. This finding is important because it expands understanding of ubiquitin's regulatory reach beyond proteins to other biomolecules. This suggests new layers of cellular regulation—ubiquitin may tag and regulate energy metabolism and other cellular processes.
