Researchers built diffusion models that combine sparse, scattered weather observations into gridded atmospheric forecasts. They created separate interfaces for radiosonde, aircraft, and surface-station data, letting one pretrained model work with all three. Adding aircraft and surface observations to radiosonde data alone reduced forecast error by 9.24%. This matters because weather observation networks are messy and unevenly distributed, so a framework that uses them all together is more accurate than picking one type.
