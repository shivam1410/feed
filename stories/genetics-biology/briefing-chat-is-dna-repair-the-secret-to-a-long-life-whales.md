DNA repair efficiency could be the secret to exceptional longevity in animals and humans. Scientists are studying whales and mole rats, animals known for long lifespans, to understand their superior DNA repair mechanisms. These long-lived mammals have evolved enhanced abilities to detect and fix DNA damage that accumulates with age. Understanding these mechanisms could unlock new therapeutic approaches for human aging and age-related diseases. This matters because if scientists identify the key DNA repair systems responsible for longevity in these animals, they might develop treatments that improve human healthspan and prevent cancer, neurodegeneration, and other age-associated conditions that limit lifespan.
