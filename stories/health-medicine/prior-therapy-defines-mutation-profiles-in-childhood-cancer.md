Chemotherapy, particularly platinum-based drugs, causes substantial and rapidly detectable genetic mutations in childhood cancers at relapse, tripling unique mutation signatures and doubling overall mutation burden. This therapy-induced mutagenesis represents an unintended consequence of cancer treatment—drugs kill cancer cells but simultaneously damage tumor DNA, accelerating evolutionary changes. These findings provide molecular evidence of how treatment itself reshapes the genetic landscape of residual cancer cells. The research suggests opportunities for developing safer, de-escalated treatment approaches that maintain efficacy while reducing therapy-induced tumor evolution and treatment-resistant relapse risk. Understanding these mutation patterns could guide more targeted, less mutagenic therapeutic strategies.
