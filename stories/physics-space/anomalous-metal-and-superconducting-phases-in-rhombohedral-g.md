Researchers observed surprising superconducting behavior in rhombohedral graphene—a specific stacking arrangement of atomic carbon layers—when placed on a tungsten diselenide (WSe2) substrate. By varying temperature, magnetic field, and electrical current, they discovered that different regions of the electrical parameter space exhibited either zero-resistance superconductivity or anomalous metallic behavior with finite saturation resistance. This means that under certain conditions, the material conducted electricity without any resistance losses, while under other conditions it behaved as an unusual metal. The coexistence of superconductivity and anomalous metallic phases suggests complex underlying electronic mechanisms. These observations indicate that rhombohedral graphene on WSe2 possesses exotic electronic properties that could have applications in next-generation quantum devices and provide insights into unconventional superconductivity mechanisms.
