Research reveals that the queen's chemical scent maintains the reproductive hierarchy in naked mole rat colonies, where a single breeding female dominates the eusocial structure. Scientists discovered that a queen-associated chemical compound functions as a biological signal actively suppressing reproduction in subordinate females. This pheromone-based control mechanism explains how colonies maintain their unusual social organization, strikingly similar to insect colonies governed by chemical signaling. Understanding this demonstrates how complex social hierarchies are governed through chemical communication that affects physiology and behavior, revealing universal mechanisms organizing social structures across animal kingdoms.
