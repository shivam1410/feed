Analysis of atmospheric measurements and climate models reveals that volcanic eruptions and extreme wildfires have systematically elevated stratospheric water vapor concentrations since 2005. Previously attributed primarily to slow tropospheric transport, these episodic aerosol injections represent a significant natural source of stratospheric water variability on decadal timescales. This mechanism has implications for atmospheric chemistry, ozone depletion, and climate, improving understanding of how transient geological and fire events shape atmospheric composition.
