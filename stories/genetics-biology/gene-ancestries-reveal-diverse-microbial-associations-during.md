Phylogenomic reconstruction of the last eukaryotic common ancestor's proteome reveals horizontal gene transfer from diverse bacterial and viral donors shaped early eukaryotic evolution. Rather than evolving solely from vertical descent, early eukaryotes acquired genetic material from many microbial sources; these foreign genes built the molecular toolkit distinguishing eukaryotes from prokaryotes. The genes encode proteins for complex cellular processes including intracellular compartmentalization, metabolism, and regulation. This evidence indicates eukaryogenesis depended critically on acquiring genes from the broader microbial world. The findings illuminate how genetic exchange between domains of life drove major evolutionary transitions.
