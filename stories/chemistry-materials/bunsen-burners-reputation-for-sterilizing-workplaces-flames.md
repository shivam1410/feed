A study challenges the long-held assumption that Bunsen burners sterilize laboratory workbenches by creating rising air currents that waft bacteria away from work surfaces. Research findings indicate the venerable device does not actually provide this protective effect as traditionally believed. This discovery has significant implications for laboratory safety protocols and contamination control practices that have relied on the presumed sterilizing effect of Bunsen burners. Labs may need to reassess decontamination procedures and implement additional microbial control measures if Bunsen burners cannot be relied upon to remove airborne bacteria from work surfaces.
