Data-driven fire-zone segmentation outperforms traditional uniform-grid approaches for short-term wildfire prediction. Rather than discretizing study areas into equal cells regardless of fire activity patterns, researchers used unsupervised segmentation combining watershed detection with K-means clustering to define prediction units directly from historical fire distribution across six French departments. Testing six different forecasting models, the fire-zone segmentation approach consistently improved prediction accuracy by 3–6% in mean IoU compared to grid-based baselines. The method is computationally lightweight, requiring less than 10 seconds per configuration, and fully parallelizable for operational deployment. This research demonstrates that how spatial data is discretized fundamentally matters more than which prediction algorithm is selected, suggesting that data-preprocessing strategy deserves equal priority with model selection in forecasting systems.
