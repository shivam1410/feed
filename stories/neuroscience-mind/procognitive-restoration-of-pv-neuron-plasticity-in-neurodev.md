Expressing the Meis2 transcription factor in hippocampal parvalbumin-expressing interneurons in adult mice with a neurodevelopmental disorder model rescues experience-dependent plasticity and improves both memory performance and network function while simultaneously suppressing spontaneous seizures. This molecular intervention restores neural mechanisms typically disrupted in developmental disorders, demonstrating that a single genetic manipulation can address multiple aspects of brain dysfunction. The results reveal that Meis2 expression in inhibitory interneurons is a key regulator of experience-dependent learning and network stability. The findings suggest Meis2 as a potential therapeutic target for restoring brain plasticity and cognitive function in neurodevelopmental conditions, while also providing seizure control.
