Researchers used Graph Attention Networks (GATs) to model spatial dependencies among 91 georeferenced soil samples for predicting microplastics and organic matter. A two-layer GAT incorporating spatial coordinates, soil properties, and land use data achieved RMSEs of 625.06 (R²=0.87) for microplastics and 0.43 (R²=0.91) for organic matter. Cross-validation revealed limited generalization, likely from small sample size and sparse graph structure. The findings demonstrate GATs' potential for spatial soil prediction while underscoring critical needs: dense datasets and improved graph connectivity. Results highlight the promise of graph-based deep learning for ecosystem health assessment and sustainable land use planning.
