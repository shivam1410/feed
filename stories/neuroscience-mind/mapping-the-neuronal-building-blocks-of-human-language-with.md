Brain recordings from awake humans identified individual neurons encoding fundamental language components at multiple complexity levels. These neurons represent grammatical relationships between words, parts of speech, and higher-order syntactic structure organizing phrases and sentences. The discovery reveals how the brain internally organizes language information from individual words up to complete sentence structure. Understanding neural language representation helps explain human speech comprehension and production. The findings provide a foundation for understanding brain mechanisms underlying language and may inform development of brain-computer interfaces that decode language from neural activity.
