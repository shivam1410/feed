DoGMA is a central-dogma-guided foundation model for multi-omics analysis in oncology that encodes biological directionality into its architecture. Traditional multi-omics models use unrestricted bidirectional attention, but DoGMA enforces directed attention reflecting central-dogma information flow as an inductive bias. The model uses a Transformer-mixture-of-experts architecture with masked hierarchical omics reconstruction pretraining to learn central-dogma-consistent interactions. Tested across cancer representation learning, survival prediction, and metastasis prediction tasks, DoGMA consistently outperforms baselines while maintaining interpretability. Ablation studies confirm that both directed attention and reconstruction-based pretraining contribute to performance gains. By aligning model architecture with biological principles, DoGMA improves transfer learning across cancer types and increases biological consistency in learned representations.
