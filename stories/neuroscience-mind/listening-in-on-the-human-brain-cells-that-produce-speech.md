Using single-neuron recordings combined with machine learning, researchers identified neurons encoding linguistic meaning and grammatical structure during speech production. Remarkably, these language-related neurons are distributed broadly across multiple brain regions rather than confined to classical language areas, suggesting a more distributed neural basis for language. These cells control how words are selected and assembled into meaningful phrases and sentences, providing mechanistic insights into language generation and possibilities for brain-computer interfaces.
