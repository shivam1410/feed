Chronic dietary cholesterol activates Ral proteins in liver cells, triggering a cascade that destroys LDLR receptors responsible for clearing cholesterol. This explains why high cholesterol paradoxically reduces the body's ability to remove cholesterol, worsening the problem. The pathway involves RalBP1, REPS1, and CTSA proteins promoting LDLR degradation. Researchers found that blocking CTSA enzyme restores LDLR levels and cholesterol clearance. This identifies a specific molecular target for treating high cholesterol and offers a potential cardiovascular disease therapy by preserving the liver's cholesterol removal capacity.
