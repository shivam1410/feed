An analysis of 70,000 biomedical studies finds that preprints—papers posted before peer review—maintain their core conclusions when later published in journals. This suggests preprints are more reliable than commonly believed, supporting their use for rapid scientific communication. The finding matters because it validates preprint servers like bioRxiv as trustworthy sources during urgent health situations or when researchers need quick dissemination.
