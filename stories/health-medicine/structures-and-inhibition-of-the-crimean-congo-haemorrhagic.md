Structural analysis of the Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever virus polymerase reveals how the virus synthesizes its RNA genome, exposing the molecular machinery underlying viral replication. This structural characterization identifies specific sites on the polymerase that drugs could target, enabling development of both nucleoside and non-nucleoside antivirals. Understanding CCHFV polymerase architecture provides a foundation for rational drug design against this severe hemorrhagic fever pathogen that causes significant human mortality with limited treatment options. The structural insights offer a pathway toward therapeutic interventions that could reduce CCHFV disease burden, exemplifying how structural virology accelerates drug development.
