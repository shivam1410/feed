Underwater volcanic activity creates distinctive dome structures on the seafloor through a novel geological process. Carbon dioxide bubbling from deep magma solidifies into solid formations reaching heights of up to five meters. This process demonstrates the interaction between volcanic gases and seafloor minerals, revealing geochemical mechanisms operating in deep-ocean environments.
