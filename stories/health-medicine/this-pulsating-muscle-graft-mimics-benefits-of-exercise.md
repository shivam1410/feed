Surgically implanted muscle grafts engineered to rhythmically contract replicated benefits of exercise in mice, producing increases in muscle mass, strength, and bone density without physical activity. This approach offers potential therapeutic value for patients unable to exercise due to immobility, aging, neurological conditions, or illness. The mechanism demonstrates that mechanical stimulation from muscular contraction alone can trigger adaptive physiological responses normally associated with voluntary exercise. Beyond direct muscle benefits, the increased muscle activity generated systemic effects including bone density improvements. This technology could provide a rehabilitation tool for sedentary populations and disease conditions limiting mobility.
