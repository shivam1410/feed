Researchers traced the biological origins of materials used in ancient Egyptian artistic decorations, including coffin decorations and wall paintings. Analysis revealed that tissue used in these artworks came from a surprisingly broad range of organisms, not from a single standardized source. This discovery provides new insights into ancient Egyptian material practices, trade networks, and cultural understanding of natural resources. Understanding which organisms were exploited for artistic purposes illuminates broader aspects of ancient Egyptian society, the ecology of that period, and the sophisticated procurement systems used to source decorative materials for tombs and temples.
