Fifty-six million years ago, a rapid rise in atmospheric carbon dioxide triggered widespread forest dieback—a climate-driven ecological collapse with parallels to modern threats. Evidence from this ancient event shows how quickly plant communities can collapse under climate stress. As CO2 levels and temperatures rose, forests that dominated for millennia suddenly died back, causing cascading ecosystem effects. This historical precedent demonstrates that today's plant communities facing rapid climate change from rising greenhouse gases face similar existential threats, highlighting the urgency of climate mitigation.
