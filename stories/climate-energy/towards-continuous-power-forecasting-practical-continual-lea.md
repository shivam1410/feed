Power forecasting models deployed in real-world markets face nonstationary data where distributions continuously evolve due to weather, infrastructure changes, and consumption shifts. Continuous Power Forecasting reframes the problem as continual learning, systematically evaluating six representative approaches across three methodological categories. Results show continual learning enables models to self-adapt to distributional drift, accumulate knowledge over time, and mitigate catastrophic forgetting without large-scale historical data storage. Better adaptive forecasting improves grid stability.
