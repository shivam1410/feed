Seismologists measured sound and seismic waves from thunderstorms—termed 'thunderquakes'—to map underground geology in regions with little natural seismic activity. By analyzing how these waves travel at varying speeds through different rock layers, researchers can survey subsurface structures without relying on earthquakes. This method enables geological mapping in previously difficult-to-survey areas, potentially revealing mineral deposits, fault lines, or subsurface hazards where traditional seismic monitoring is unavailable.
