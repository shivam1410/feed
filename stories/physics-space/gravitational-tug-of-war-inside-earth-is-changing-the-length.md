The molten core tugs on Earth. Churning motion in the outer core creates gravitational forces that speed up and slow down the planet's rotation by tiny amounts. The tug-of-war between core and crust explains wobbles in how long a day lasts. This matters because it connects deep planetary physics to surface timekeeping.
