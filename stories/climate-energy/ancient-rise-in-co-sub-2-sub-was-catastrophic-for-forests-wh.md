A widespread forest dieback occurred 56 million years ago when atmospheric CO2 rose rapidly during a period of climate change. This ancient ecological collapse shows striking parallels to today's climate-driven forest stress. Understanding how forests responded to past CO2 surges provides crucial historical precedent for predicting how current forests may respond to ongoing anthropogenic climate change and continued atmospheric CO2 increases.
