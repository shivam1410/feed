High-energy electron scattering experiments performed on three different nuclei revealed that short-range nucleon pairing—a fundamental quantum phenomenon where pairs of nucleons interact at very close distances—depends far more strongly on the specific quantum orbitals occupied by nucleons than existing theoretical models predicted. The experimental results demonstrate the crucial role that nuclear shell structure plays in governing these short-range pairing correlations. Why it matters: This finding refines our fundamental understanding of nuclear forces and structure, indicating that current theoretical models require significant revision to accurately describe how nucleons interact at short ranges, with implications for nuclear astrophysics and fundamental physics.
