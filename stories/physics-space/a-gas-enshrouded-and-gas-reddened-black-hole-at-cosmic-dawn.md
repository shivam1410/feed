Astronomers observed a distant supermassive black hole from the early universe surrounded by dense gas, providing insight into rapid black hole growth shortly after the Big Bang. The gas-enshrouded state appears to fuel rapid mass accumulation through disk accretion, allowing black holes to reach enormous sizes within the universe's first billion years—faster than previously thought possible. This observation may explain mysterious dim, reddish galaxies nicknamed 'little red dots,' suggesting they harbor heavily obscured, gas-enshrouded black holes powering vigorous growth during cosmic dawn. These findings reshape models of early supermassive black hole formation and evolution.
