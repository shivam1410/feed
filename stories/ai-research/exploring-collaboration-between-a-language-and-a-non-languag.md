Chess collaboration tasks show that integrating subagent representations directly into language models via learned state tokens significantly outperforms text-based verbalization of subagent information. This direct state representation approach scales effectively with task complexity. This matters because it reveals language models operate more efficiently with structured representations than natural language, suggesting better architectures for multi-agent AI systems.
