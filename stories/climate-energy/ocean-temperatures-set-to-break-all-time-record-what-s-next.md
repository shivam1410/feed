Ocean surface temperatures are expected to reach all-time record highs with cascading ecological consequences. Elevated sea temperatures weaken the ocean's capacity to absorb atmospheric carbon dioxide—reducing an important natural climate regulator—and simultaneously stress coral reefs through thermal damage. These impacts create coupled climate feedbacks: as carbon sinks weaken, atmospheric CO2 accumulates faster, driving further warming. Tropical coral ecosystems face existential threats from heat stress. Record ocean temperatures underscore the urgent imperative to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and stabilize the global climate system.
