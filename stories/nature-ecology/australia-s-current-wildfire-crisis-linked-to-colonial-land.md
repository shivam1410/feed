A 1,000-year lake-sediment record from southeastern Australia reveals how Indigenous cultural burning maintained open woodlands. Colonial disruption ended this practice, and dense forest replaced the sparse landscape. Now climate change ignites these fuel-heavy forests into catastrophic wildfires. This matters because it shows today's fire crisis has historical roots in colonial land-use change.
