Researchers introduce MSR-IVA, a method fusing brain structure with dynamic functional connectivity for disease understanding. The technique maintains a shared structural representation across brain states while allowing state-specific adaptations, handling subjects who don't express all states. On Alzheimer's Disease data, MSR-IVA achieved 6.5% improvement in source coupling and 15.7% reduction in unmatched dependence versus independent pairwise analysis. Cross-state structural correlation improved dramatically: 0.9177 for MSR-IVA versus 0.2978 without sharing. This controlled sharing preserves brain structure correspondence while capturing state-specific relationships, enabling better understanding of how brain anatomy relates to changing functional networks.
