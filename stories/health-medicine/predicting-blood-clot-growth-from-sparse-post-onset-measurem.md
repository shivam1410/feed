A latent neural differential equations framework infers unknown blood-clotting parameters from sparse early measurements and forecasts thrombosis progression. The computational model combines the coagulation cascade with diffusion physics to govern clot growth, but patient-specific measurements are difficult to obtain. Testing seven probabilistic methods including stochastic neural ordinary differential equations (SNODE), stochastic neural functional differential equations (SNFDE), neural process baselines, and traditional Gompertz-curve retrieval, SNODE achieved best performance. Using four known biochemical inputs (fibrinogen, factors IX, VIII, V) with sparse early clot-size observations, SNODE successfully inferred the tissue-factor parameter and predicted subsequent clot growth. Prediction accuracy improved as more observations became available. This framework bridges computational blood-clotting models with real sparse clinical data, enabling practical thrombosis prediction.
