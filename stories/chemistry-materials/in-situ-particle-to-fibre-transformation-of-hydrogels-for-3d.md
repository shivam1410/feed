A three-dimensional printing method transforms hydrogel particles into aligned microfibers during the extrusion process, creating structural anisotropy that guides tissue growth. This controlled alignment of fibers accelerates muscle tissue regeneration compared to isotropic hydrogels. The printing technique creates biomaterials with directional properties. This matters because controlling material structure during manufacturing enhances biological performance of tissue engineered constructs, advancing regenerative medicine applications and improving outcomes for muscle tissue repair and replacement therapies.
