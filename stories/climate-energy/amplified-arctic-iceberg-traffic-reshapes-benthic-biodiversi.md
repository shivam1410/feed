Accelerated Arctic glacier melting is increasing icebergs calving into the ocean. These icebergs deliver rocks (dropstones) to deep-sea floors, reshaping benthic habitats far from glaciers. The rocks provide substrate for attachment and create ecological structures in otherwise featureless deep-ocean sediments. Accelerating iceberg discharge and changing sea ice patterns are shifting dropstone delivery patterns. This extends climate change impacts far beyond glacial regions into remote deep-ocean ecosystems. This matters because it reveals unexpected mechanisms by which climate change affects deep-sea biodiversity in extreme environments where impacts were previously underappreciated and demonstrates climate change has cascading consequences extending to Earth's most remote ecosystems.
