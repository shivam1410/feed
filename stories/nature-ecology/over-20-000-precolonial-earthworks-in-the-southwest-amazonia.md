LiDAR (light detection and ranging) data from southwestern Amazonia reveals that the northern portion alone contains approximately 20,000 precolonial earthworks—matching the total previously estimated for the entire Amazonian region. These earthworks represent ancient settlements, ceremonial sites, and modified landscapes created by indigenous peoples before European contact. The discovery dramatically revises population density and cultural sophistication estimates, indicating substantially greater precolonial development. Remote sensing reveals archaeological signatures beneath forest canopy, reshaping understanding of precolonial American civilizations and challenging narratives of sparse indigenous settlement.
