Researchers proposed MotionVLA, a vision-language-action model for humanoid motion generation using a dual-stream frequency tokenizer architecture. The model separately encodes pose, or body position and configuration, and physical dynamics, or movement forces and physics, achieving better diversity and consistency compared to single-codebook approaches. This separation reflects the distinct nature of kinematic structure versus underlying physical behavior. The autoregressive architecture generates motion sequences sequentially. Testing shows improvements in both diversity and consistency. This advances humanoid robotics motion generation.
