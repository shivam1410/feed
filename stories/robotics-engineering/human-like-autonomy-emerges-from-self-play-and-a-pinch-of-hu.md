Self-play reinforcement learning can train autonomous driving policies aligned with human behavior using minimal human demonstrations. Researchers used just 30 minutes of human driving data as regularization—2,500 times less than traditional imitation learning. The resulting policy coordinates with held-out human trajectories and completed training in 15 hours on a consumer-grade GPU. By treating human data as lightweight regularization rather than primary training, this method achieves human-like driving conventions without expensive datasets, opening possibilities for efficiently training aligned autonomous systems at scale.
