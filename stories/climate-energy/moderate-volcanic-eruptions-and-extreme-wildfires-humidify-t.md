Volcanic eruptions and extreme wildfires inject aerosols into the stratosphere where they persist for months, catalyzing stratospheric water vapor production. Satellite observations and climate models show that since 2005, these episodic events have systematically elevated stratospheric humidity levels. Accumulating stratospheric water vapor absorbs heat and perturbs ozone and temperature dynamics. The finding reveals that episodic disturbances—not only steady-state conditions—drive significant atmospheric changes. Climate models must incorporate these events to predict future atmospheric conditions accurately.
