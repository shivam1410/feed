This work argues that agent safety should be enforced as a runtime contract through preventive controls and verifiable evidence of safe execution, rather than relying exclusively on training-time alignment methods. Runtime enforcement creates an additional safety layer that catches failures in deployment, complementing methods used during model development. The runtime contract approach treats safety as a system-level property actively enforced during operation, similar to safety contracts in software systems. This shifts focus from perfect training-time alignment to practical safety guarantees during deployment.
