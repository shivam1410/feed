A study reveals that chemotherapy, particularly platinum-based drugs, induces substantial genetic mutations in childhood cancers that recur after treatment. Cancer cells surviving platinum chemotherapy exhibit tripled mutational signatures and doubled overall mutation burden compared to untreated tumors. This therapy-induced mutagenesis accelerates tumor evolution, potentially promoting treatment resistance and disease relapse. Understanding these effects highlights opportunities for developing safer treatment protocols that minimize harmful genetic changes while maintaining cancer control.
