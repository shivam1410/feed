Scientists directly observed a mid-ocean ridge—where tectonic plates spread apart and new oceanic crust forms—widen in real time, recording several meters of seafloor motion and massive lava outflows. This marks the first direct observation of active seafloor spreading. Mid-ocean ridges generate most new crust on Earth through continuous volcanic activity as plates diverge. Prior understanding came from indirect evidence like magnetic patterns and seismic data. This direct observation validates plate tectonic theory's predictions and provides unprecedented detail about crustal formation.
