The enzyme ArmA from Methanobrevibacter smithii, a dominant human gut microbe, cleaves archaeal cell walls in a way that revealed an unexpected structure—archaeal peptidoglycan contains a previously unknown sugar. This structural surprise deepens understanding of microbial cell biology and the evolutionary history of the ancient archaea domain. It expands knowledge of human microbiome physiology.
