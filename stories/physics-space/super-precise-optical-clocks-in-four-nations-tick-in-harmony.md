Researchers successfully compared seven super-precise optical clocks distributed across four European nations using signals transmitted over fiber-optic cables. Each device represents one of the world's most accurate timekeeping instruments, capable of measuring time to extraordinary precision. Synchronizing these clocks across international distances demonstrates the possibility of coordinated, highly precise timekeeping at continental scale. Such synchronized atomic clocks enable fundamental physics tests—checking whether time flows identically everywhere or whether fundamental constants vary—and support emerging technologies requiring coordinated precision timing. This achievement validates the reliability and synchronization of optical clock technology and paves the way for potential international networks of atomic reference standards.
