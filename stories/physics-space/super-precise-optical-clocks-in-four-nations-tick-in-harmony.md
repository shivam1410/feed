Seven ultra-precise optical clocks distributed across four European nations were compared by transmitting signals over fiber-optic cables, demonstrating unprecedented synchronization and agreement among the devices. This international comparison test validates the accuracy and stability of the latest generation of atomic clocks. Such super-precise timekeeping capabilities enable fundamental physics research testing theories of relativity, support quantum technologies, and establish time standards for scientific applications. Synchronizing optical clocks across international borders and distances represents a technological advance toward establishing a global network of precision time sources for coordinated research.
