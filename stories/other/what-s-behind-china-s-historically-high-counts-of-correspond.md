Data from 2016 to 2020 reveals nearly one-third of papers by Chinese researchers listed multiple corresponding authors—substantially exceeding international norms. This pattern reflected specific policy incentives encouraging multiple author designations in leadership roles. However, policy reforms implemented in China are expected to reduce this practice, gradually realigning Chinese authorship conventions with international standards. These reforms recognize multiple corresponding author designations can complicate accountability and attribution clarity in the peer review process.
