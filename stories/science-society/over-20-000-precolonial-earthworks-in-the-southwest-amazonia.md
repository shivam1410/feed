Using canopy-penetrating LiDAR technology, researchers discovered that the northern part of southwestern Amazonia contains over 20,000 precolonial earthworks. This single region contains as many ancient structures as previously estimated for the entire Amazon basin. The earthworks—likely residential, ceremonial, or agricultural features—reveal far more extensive precolonial settlement and engineering than previously recognized. LiDAR's ability to see through dense forest canopy has revolutionized archaeological understanding of the Amazon. This discovery fundamentally revises estimates of precolonial Amazonian population size, complexity, and civilization, demonstrating that ancient Amazonian societies were far more sophisticated than earlier scholarship suggested.
