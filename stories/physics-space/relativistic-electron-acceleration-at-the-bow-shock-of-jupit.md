NASA's Juno spacecraft observed relativistic electrons accelerating at Jupiter's bow shock—the shock wave where the solar wind encounters the planet's magnetosphere. These observations revealed a universal scaling law connecting shock size to the maximum energy of accelerated cosmic rays. This relationship appears to hold across different astrophysical shock environments, suggesting a fundamental principle governing particle acceleration throughout the cosmos. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial: it explains cosmic ray origins, informs solar energetic particle models, and illuminates how the universe accelerates particles to extreme energies. Jupiter's bow shock serves as a natural laboratory for studying extreme plasma physics.
