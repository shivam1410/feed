Researchers leveraged AlphaFold3 computational predictions of molecular contacts within CRISPR genome-editing enzyme complexes to systematically improve the enzyme's selectivity for intended target DNA sequences. By precisely identifying which amino-acid residues make direct contact with target DNA, researchers computationally redesigned the enzyme to discriminate more effectively between target sequences and similar off-target sequences throughout the genome. This structure-guided rational engineering approach substantially reduced unintended edits at off-target sites—a critical challenge that currently undermines CRISPR therapeutic development and safety. The research demonstrates how computational structure prediction can effectively inform rational enzyme engineering for dramatically improved specificity and selectivity. Such advances significantly enhance CRISPR's translational potential as a precise therapeutic tool by substantially reducing off-target mutations that could cause harmful adverse side effects in patients.
