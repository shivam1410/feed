Although Antarctic ice loss projections diverge widely toward 2100, present-day melting rates reliably forecast mid-century sea level rise. This allows governments and coastal communities to plan infrastructure and adaptation strategies for coming decades with confidence. Current rates robustly predict impacts around 2050, providing a firm foundation for near-term climate adaptation planning. However, longer-term projections toward century's end remain substantially uncertain, requiring improved models to resolve differences in end-of-century scenarios. This distinction between confident near-term prediction and uncertain long-term projections has important implications for coastal planning and policy.
