Alzheimer's disease produces consistent cellular and molecular changes across different racial and ethnic populations. Single-cell sequencing of brain tissue from deceased individuals—African American, Latin, and white—revealed specific cell types show parallel molecular disruptions in Alzheimer's. These signatures include changes in genes related to inflammation, neurodegeneration, and protein aggregation. The patterns hold across ancestry groups despite known health disparities in disease prevalence. Discovery of shared molecular signatures suggests common disease mechanisms regardless of genetic background and supports targeting conserved pathways for therapeutics.
