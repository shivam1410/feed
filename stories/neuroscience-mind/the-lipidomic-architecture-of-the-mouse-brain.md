A spatial lipidomic atlas identified over 500 distinct biochemical territories in the mouse brain that correlate with cell types and neural connectivity patterns, reveal oligodendrocyte diversity and ventricular organization, and shift during pregnancy. Lipids are not uniformly distributed but form a complex chemical geography mirroring brain architecture. This suggests lipids organize neural function and contribute to brain plasticity.
