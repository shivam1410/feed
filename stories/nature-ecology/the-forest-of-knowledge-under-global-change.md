The Amazon rainforest's biocultural heritage—plant species, ecosystem services, and Indigenous cultural knowledge—faces serious threats from climate change. The forest moderates regional climate, stores carbon, and provides medicinal and food plants supporting both ecosystems and Indigenous communities. Climate change threatens to alter temperature and precipitation patterns, shifting where plants can survive and destabilizing relationships between ecosystems and Indigenous peoples who have sustainably managed these forests for millennia. Losing this would eliminate both irreplaceable biodiversity and Indigenous knowledge systems.
