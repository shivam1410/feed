Female treefrogs select mates less carefully when exposed to large choruses of calling males, a phenomenon driven by overwhelming mating options. The abundance of potential partners impairs females' ability to discriminate between males based on call quality and mate-selection criteria. This reveals how acoustic competition and sensory overload can disrupt reproductive behavior in species relying on sound-based mate choice, with implications for understanding communication in nature.
