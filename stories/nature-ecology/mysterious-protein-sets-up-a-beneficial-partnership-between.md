Beneficial bacteria living inside aphids produce a protein called SyeA that enables their symbiosis with their host insects. This protein structurally resembles proteins made by disease-causing pathogens, suggesting the partnership between Buchnera bacteria and aphids originated as a pathogenic relationship that later became mutually beneficial—revealing how some modern symbioses may have evolved from ancient infections.
