Potentially fraudulent papers published in predatory journals receive roughly twice as many citations as genuine papers, often citing each other in networks. This inflated citation behavior artificially boosts journal impact factors. The finding exposes how paper mills corrupt academic metrics and undermine the reliability of citation-based quality measures.
