Glioblastoma tumors and their immune-suppressive microenvironment both express GPNMB, a surface protein. Multi-omic profiling identified this shared antigen as a dual therapeutic target. CAR-T cells engineered to recognize GPNMB effectively killed both tumor cells and tumor-associated myeloid cells in laboratory experiments and animal models, enhancing anti-tumor immunity. This dual-targeting approach overcomes a critical immunotherapy limitation: tumor microenvironments actively suppress anti-cancer immune responses. The strategy demonstrates that targeting shared antigens can strengthen anti-cancer immunity.
