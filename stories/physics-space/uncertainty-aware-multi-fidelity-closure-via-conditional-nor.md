Researchers formulated reduced-order model (ROM) closure—the problem of capturing effects of truncated scales on resolved scales—as multi-fidelity learning. They proposed an uncertainty-aware framework using conditional normalizing flows to learn probabilistic mappings from low-fidelity ROM coefficients to high-fidelity ones. Tested on vortex merging governed by 2D Navier-Stokes equations, residual learning outperformed direct learning. This approach improves ROM accuracy while quantifying uncertainty, critical for engineering applications relying on physics surrogates for thermal management and battery systems.
