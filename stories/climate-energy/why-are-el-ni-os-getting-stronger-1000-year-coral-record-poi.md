El Niño events are intensifying, and human-caused climate warming is demonstrably driving the trend. Researchers reconstructed 1,000 years of Pacific Ocean sea surface temperatures using detailed coral records, revealing that rising temperatures linked to greenhouse gas emissions are amplifying El Niño strength and frequency. This millennium-long historical perspective substantially strengthens the evidence connecting human activity to increasingly powerful disruptions of ocean circulation patterns. The finding matters critically because El Niños dramatically disrupt weather globally—affecting rainfall, temperature, and agricultural productivity—so stronger events will amplify climate risks for billions of people worldwide.
