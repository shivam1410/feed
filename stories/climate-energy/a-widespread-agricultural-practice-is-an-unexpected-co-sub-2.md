In the Mississippi River basin, 'liming'—adding calcium compounds to reduce soil acidity—unexpectedly resulted in net atmospheric carbon capture despite long being considered a carbon dioxide source. The finding challenges assumptions about liming's carbon footprint and suggests this widespread agricultural practice may actually help mitigate climate change by sequestering soil carbon, offering dual benefits of soil improvement and carbon management.
