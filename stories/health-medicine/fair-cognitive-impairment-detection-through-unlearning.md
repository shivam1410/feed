Mild cognitive impairment detection from speech could enable scalable screening, but deployed models often exploit demographic shortcuts—performing well on majority groups but poorly on minorities. Researchers developed a multimodal framework combining speech, text, and image information with demographic unlearning using gradient reversal, which discourages the shared representation from encoding demographic attributes. Evaluated on multilingual benchmarks (TAUKADIAL and PREPARE), the method achieves state-of-the-art MCI classification while substantially reducing performance gaps across sex and language. Transfer analysis shows demographic unlearning produces more robust representations that generalize across datasets. This approach demonstrates that fairness and robustness improvements can be achieved simultaneously through representation-level demographic removal.
