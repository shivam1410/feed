Radial glia progenitor lineage tracing in stem-cell-derived cortical organoids reveals highly plastic proliferation patterns and notably reduced clonal neuronal diversity compared to in vivo brain development. The analysis demonstrates that non-cell-autonomous niche cues—environmental signals present in the developing brain but absent in organoids—are essential for proper temporal control of cortical lineage progression. The findings highlight how organoids, despite their utility, lack critical environmental factors that regulate the timing and diversity of neural cell types. These missing signals likely derive from interactions with other cell types, diffusible factors, and physical cues in the native brain microenvironment. Addressing these gaps is critical for developing organoid systems that faithfully recapitulate developmental processes.
