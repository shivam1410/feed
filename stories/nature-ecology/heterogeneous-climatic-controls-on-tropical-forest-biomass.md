Analysis of aboveground biomass patterns across three major tropical forest regions reveals heterogeneous relationships between climate and forest carbon storage. The findings demonstrate that simple climate-biomass relationships do not transfer across regions; instead, complex interactions among climate, topography, and soils determine forest structure and carbon capacity. Each region shows distinct climatic controls, emphasizing the importance of regional context. These insights are critical for accurately predicting how climate change will affect tropical forest carbon sequestration capacity and regional variation in climate impacts on forest ecosystems.
