Chiral perovskite superlattices—crystalline materials with alternating layers of different compositions—exhibit circularly polarized superfluorescence at room temperature. Superfluorescence occurs when excited atoms emit intense, coherent light. The emission intensity and polarization can be controlled using weak magnetic fields, enabling precise optical tuning without requiring extreme temperatures or field strengths, with potential applications in quantum optics and optical devices.
