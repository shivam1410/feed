Xaira Therapeutics' X-Cell model reveals a fundamental principle for AI-driven drug discovery: information-limited data is the bottleneck, not computational capacity. Their 3.1B parameter model hit a scaling plateau—test loss flatlined while training loss continued dropping—exposing insufficient data diversity. By expanding their dataset approximately 30-fold with information-rich gene expression data, they unlocked proper scaling with additional parameters and compute. This challenges the prevailing assumption that bigger models and more compute solve everything. The finding underscores that biological research requires rich, diverse training data. Xaira's teams invested tens of millions in data collection and experimental infrastructure, suggesting that information abundance beats brute-force scaling for predictive genomics and drug development.
