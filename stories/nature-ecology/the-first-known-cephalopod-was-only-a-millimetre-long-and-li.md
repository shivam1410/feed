Eoceras, the earliest known cephalopod, lived approximately 520 million years ago during the early Cambrian period and measured only about one millimeter in length. Fossil discoveries reveal this primitive cephalopod possessed a siphuncle—a specialized anatomical structure that modern squid and cuttlefish use for buoyancy control and swimming. The presence of a siphuncle in such an ancient, microscopic organism indicates that cephalopods emerged and differentiated earlier than previously thought, during the explosive diversification of animal life in the Cambrian. The findings push back the fossil record of this major animal group and suggest sophisticated anatomical features developed rapidly in early cephalopod evolution. This discovery fundamentally revises understanding of cephalopod origins, early marine animal diversification, and the pace of evolution during the Cambrian explosion. The tiny fossil provides crucial evidence for early metazoan complexity.
