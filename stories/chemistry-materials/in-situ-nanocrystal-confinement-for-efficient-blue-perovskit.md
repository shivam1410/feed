Researchers achieved highly efficient blue perovskite light-emitting diodes with an external quantum efficiency of 21.8% through in situ polymerization-driven nanocrystal confinement. This technique controls crystal growth at the nanoscale, improving light emission and device stability. The breakthrough addresses long-standing challenges in perovskite LED development, particularly achieving efficient blue emission for displays and lighting applications. Perovskite materials offer advantages over conventional semiconductors: lower manufacturing temperatures and costs. This 21.8% efficiency represents significant progress toward practical perovskite displays and highlights nanocrystal engineering as key to improving optoelectronic device performance.
