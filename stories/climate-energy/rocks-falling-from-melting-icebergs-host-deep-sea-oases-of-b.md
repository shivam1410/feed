Melting Arctic icebergs drop rocks onto deep seafloor habitats, creating unexpected oases for deep-sea organisms. These dropstones—rocks carried by icebergs and released as ice melts—provide hard substrate and habitat structure in otherwise sparse, soft-sediment deep-ocean regions. They enable colonization by corals, sponges, and other Arctic-adapted animals that might otherwise lack suitable habitat. This ripple effect of climate change provides biodiversity refugia in extreme environments. This matters because it reveals how climate change can create novel ecosystems with consequences for deep-sea biodiversity previously unanticipated, demonstrating that climate impacts extend beyond simple loss to sometimes create new ecological opportunities.
