Certain tree species commonly planted in urban environments release chemical compounds that contribute to ground-level ozone formation, thereby degrading air quality despite intentions to improve it through reforestation. Rising temperatures are projected to exacerbate this pollution-generating effect. This finding challenges the assumption that more trees universally benefit city air quality, suggesting instead that tree species selection requires careful consideration of emissions profiles. Urban planners may need to reconsider reforestation strategies to account for species-specific air quality impacts.
