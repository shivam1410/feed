While Antarctic ice loss projections diverge widely for 2100, current melting rates robustly predict sea-level rise by mid-century, providing reliable basis for near-term planning. Present-day observational data constrains mid-century sea levels despite uncertainty about longer-term ice sheet stability. This separates actionable near-term predictions from speculative long-term scenarios. Coastal infrastructure planning can rely on established current trends while highlighting need for improved climate models to resolve uncertainties beyond mid-century about Antarctica's ultimate contribution to sea-level rise.
