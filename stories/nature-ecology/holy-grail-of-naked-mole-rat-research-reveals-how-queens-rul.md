A simple chemical compound called isopropyl myristate, produced by naked mole-rat queens, maintains reproductive dominance within the colony through chemical communication. High-ranking colony members detect this odorant and avoid it, triggering hormonal changes in other females that suppress breeding capacity. This discovery reveals how a single pheromone orchestrates the extreme eusocial hierarchy characteristic of these unusual mammals, where reproductive roles are rigidly determined. The finding provides crucial insight into how mammalian societies achieve colony-level organization.
