Human brain organoids cultured for more than five years continued maturing and recording the passage of time, following human-specific developmental programs despite existing outside a living body. Brain cells in these organoids demonstrated ongoing maturation patterns consistent with in vivo development. This reveals that critical aspects of brain development and timing mechanisms are intrinsic to neural tissue itself, not dependent on external body signals. The finding has implications for understanding human neurodevelopment and modeling neurological diseases in vitro. It suggests that organized neural tissue can maintain developmental momentum in culture over years.
