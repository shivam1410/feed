A moiré system composed of Bernal bilayer graphene and rhombohedral tetralayer graphene exhibits a rich variety of both integer and fractional high-Chern insulators. These exotic quantum states arise from the interference patterns created by misaligned graphene layers. High-Chern insulators are topological states with robust quantum properties. The observations demonstrate that twisted graphene systems can generate multiple types of topological states. These findings advance understanding of quantum materials and topological physics. The discovery opens pathways for engineering exotic quantum states in graphene systems and potential applications in quantum devices and fundamental quantum physics research.
