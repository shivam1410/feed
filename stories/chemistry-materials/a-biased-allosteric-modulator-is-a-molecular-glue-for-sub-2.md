AP-7-168, a derivative of a β-arrestin-biased allosteric modulator, acts as a "molecular glue" that stabilizes the dimerization of β2-adrenergic receptors by promoting the association of two β2AR proteins into a stable complex. This compound works through a novel mechanism enabling more targeted therapeutic effects than traditional drug approaches. The discovery reveals a new mechanism for modulating GPCR function and has implications for drug design, as stabilizing specific protein-protein interactions can enable more precise therapeutic interventions. This approach represents an innovative strategy for developing drugs that act through receptor assembly rather than traditional inhibition or activation.
