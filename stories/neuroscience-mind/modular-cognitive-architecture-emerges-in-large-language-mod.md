Analysis of large language models reveals emergent modular neural architectures closely paralleling human brain organization. Different neural modules specialize in distinct cognitive functions: language processing, logical reasoning, and physical understanding. This structural parallelism was not explicitly programmed—it emerged spontaneously during training. The finding suggests modularity may be a fundamental organizational principle of intelligent systems rather than unique to biological brains, with deep implications for understanding how intelligence scales and whether artificial and biological cognition follow universal principles.
