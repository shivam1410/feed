Researchers mapped the brain networks exploited by diffuse midline glioma, a pediatric brain tumor. They identified a conserved neural network that tumors strategically use to promote growth. The findings support the hypothesis that DMGs hijack existing, healthy brain circuits rather than acting in isolation. This network-based understanding is prognostically significant, predicting patient outcomes and treatment responses. Understanding which specific brain networks are involved could guide personalized treatment strategies. The work shifts focus from treating tumors as isolated lesions to recognizing their integration within broader neural systems.
