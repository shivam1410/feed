Physics-informed machine learning combines standard neural networks with domain constraints to enforce physically plausible predictions. A physics-informed LSTM for flood prediction incorporates hydrological constraints through a Trend Alignment penalty in the loss function ensuring discharge trends match precipitation trends. In data-scarce settings using only 5 percent of available training data, the physics-informed model improved Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency from 0.20 to 0.23 compared to baseline LSTM. The model maintains stable behavior and directional consistency under extreme climate scenarios where baseline LSTM produces physically impossible predictions. Domain knowledge integration improves robustness without complex equations.
