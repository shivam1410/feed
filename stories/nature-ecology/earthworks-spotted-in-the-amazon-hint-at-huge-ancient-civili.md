Nearly 400 earthwork structures discovered in the Amazon suggest the Aquiry civilization was far larger and more sophisticated than previously thought. The findings indicate substantial population centers existed in regions previously considered sparsely inhabited, potentially requiring a major reassessment of pre-Columbian Amazonian societies. Additionally, newly discovered tiny fossils represent the earliest known squid ancestors, extending understanding of mollusc evolution. Together, these discoveries demonstrate that significant ancient civilizations and evolutionary lineages remain hidden in relatively well-studied regions. They highlight how remote sensing and careful excavation continue to reveal previously unknown chapters of human and natural history.
