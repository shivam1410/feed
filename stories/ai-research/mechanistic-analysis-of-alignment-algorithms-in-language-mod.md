Researchers analyzed six preference-optimization alignment methods (PPO, DPO, SimPO, ORPO, GRPO, KTO) using mechanistic tools like layer-wise linear probing, sparse autoencoders, and crosscoders across three open-weight model families. They found that preference signals consistently concentrate in early-mid or mid-late layers, but different optimization objectives induce qualitatively distinct geometric transformations in latent space. KTO and GRPO enhance linear separability through constructive feature sharing and sparse recruitment, while DPO and ORPO degrade separability via non-constructive geometric rotation and feature attenuation. This reveals alignment as a heterogeneous internal intervention, not uniform restructuring.
