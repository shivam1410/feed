The T7 bacteriophage uses its kinase enzyme T7K to phosphorylate nearly all bacterial and viral proteins during infection, systematically disarming bacterial defense systems. This remarkably broad phosphorylation strategy allows the phage to overcome the host's immune mechanisms. By chemically modifying so many proteins simultaneously, T7K creates a cellular environment where bacterial defenses fail. This discovery reveals an elegant viral strategy for infection and advances understanding of the evolutionary arms race between phages and bacteria. The findings have potential implications for phage therapy and antibiotic development strategies.
