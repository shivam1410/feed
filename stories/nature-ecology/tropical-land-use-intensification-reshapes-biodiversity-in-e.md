Farming is intensifying in some of the world's most biodiverse regions, as agricultural demand has grown over recent decades. Researchers assessed about 40,000 species in Sumatra, Indonesia. They ranged from soil microbes to vertebrates. The study found that intensification threatens biodiversity through species loss and changes in which species share a community. The effects show up at different spatial scales.
