In extreme magnetic fields near a magnetar, empty space itself bends and polarizes light—a phenomenon called vacuum birefringence that quantum theory predicts. Detailed polarization measurements of the magnetar 1E 1547.0−5408 show X-ray emission patterns consistent with this effect, providing the first direct observational evidence for quantum electrodynamics predictions in such extreme conditions. This groundbreaking finding demonstrates that empty space isn't truly empty but actively interacts with electromagnetic radiation. The discovery strengthens our understanding of physics in the universe's most extreme environments and opens new windows into quantum phenomena.
