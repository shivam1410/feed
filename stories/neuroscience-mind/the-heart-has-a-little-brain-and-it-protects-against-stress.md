The heart contains its own localized neural network distinct from the central nervous system. Experiments in mice showed that specific cardiac neurons prevent the heart from malfunctioning during stress. This 'little brain' in the heart operates semi-autonomously, regulating heartbeat and protecting against stress-induced arrhythmias through local neural circuitry. The finding reveals an intrinsic cardioprotective mechanism and highlights the heart's sophistication beyond pump function, opening therapeutic avenues for stress-related conditions.
