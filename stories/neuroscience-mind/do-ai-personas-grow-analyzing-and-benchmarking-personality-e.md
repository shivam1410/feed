Personality-conditioned large language model agents exhibit event-induced trait shifts that qualitatively match human psychological responses but with smaller magnitudes and more compressed variance. Testing personality-driven agents through simulated life events, researchers observed shifts in Big Five personality traits in expected directions—conscientiousness increases with achievement events, openness increases with learning events. However, agent trait shifts show reduced amplitude compared to human population data, and personality variance compresses after events. The work introduces a new benchmark for evaluating directional fidelity in personality change. These findings reveal gaps between human and AI personality dynamics, suggesting that achieving realistic personality evolution in long-horizon agents requires architectural innovations beyond current personality-conditioning approaches.
