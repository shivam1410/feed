Graph neural networks for protein modeling typically rely on sequence adjacency or geometric proximity, capturing only partial folding principles. This work introduces a secondary-structure-aware GNN augmenting node representations with secondary structure assignments (α-helices, β-sheets) and constructing edges from energy-filtered hydrogen-bond interactions. This captures local structural context and long-range couplings critical to protein stability. Evaluation on standard benchmarks shows consistent improvements over existing methods. Learned representations offer enhanced biological interpretability—connectivity patterns align with established structural motifs, making reasoning transparent to protein scientists.
