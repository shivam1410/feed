Single-atom precision measurements performed across a continuous phase transition reveal that order-parameter fluctuations follow non-Gaussian statistical distributions rather than the Gaussian distributions traditionally assumed in theoretical frameworks and predictions. These observations demonstrate critical scaling consistent with established phase transition theory, yet demonstrate conclusively that complete statistical distributions—encompassing more than average values—are essential for genuine understanding of phase transitions and universality principles. The experimental findings challenge simplified theoretical models that treat phase transitions as Gaussian stochastic processes. Understanding the complete distribution of fluctuations beyond mean values may substantially refine comprehensive theories of critical phenomena and improve predictive accuracy in condensed-matter physics systems. This research highlights the fundamental importance of measuring beyond traditional averages when studying quantum systems at critical points.
