Researchers demonstrated that nine-residue peptides—short chains of amino acids—can encode discrete interaction patterns that direct the formation of hexagonal pore structures. When combined, these peptides hierarchically tile together into multichannel nanofibrils with precisely defined three-dimensional topology. The remarkable aspect of this work is that the entire structural blueprint is encoded in the amino acid sequence itself, allowing the peptides to self-assemble into these complex geometries. The nanofibrils are laterally expandable, meaning they can grow larger while maintaining their ordered architecture. This work illustrates how simple peptide sequences can program sophisticated supramolecular assembly, offering potential applications in biomaterials, drug delivery, and nanotechnology. The achievement demonstrates the power of sequence-based design principles in synthetic biology.
