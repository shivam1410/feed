Scientists demonstrated optical cooling in stacked two-dimensional semiconductor materials where light triggers phonon-assisted interfacial charge transfer. The mechanism releases heat energy through vibrations without requiring cryogenic liquids or demanding stringent quantum-efficiency constraints typical of earlier optical cooling methods. This approach enables compact, passive cooling systems suitable for miniaturized electronic and photonic devices. The technique has implications for thermal management in nanotechnology and advanced computing, potentially reducing cooling infrastructure complexity in systems currently relying on bulky refrigeration.
