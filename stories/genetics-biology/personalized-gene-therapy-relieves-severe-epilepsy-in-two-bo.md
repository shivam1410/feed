Personalized gene therapy proved effective for treating severe epilepsy in two boys, with one child achieving independent walking for the first time—a significant functional improvement. The therapy works by switching off one copy of a disease-causing gene, reducing the genetic load driving seizures. This represents a substantial clinical advancement, demonstrating that tailored genetic interventions can provide dramatic benefits for patients with otherwise intractable epilepsy who haven't responded to conventional treatments. The finding highlights the potential of gene therapy as a precision medicine approach for severe neurological disorders, opening new possibilities for similar targeted interventions in other genetic epilepsies.
