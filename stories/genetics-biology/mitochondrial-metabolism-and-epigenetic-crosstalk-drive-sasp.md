In aging cells, mitochondria-derived acetyl-CoA promotes histone acetylation and increases chromatin accessibility at inflammatory genes, driving the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP). Inhibiting SLC25A1, a mitochondrial transporter, attenuates these effects. This reveals how mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetics interact to drive age-related dysfunction, suggesting therapeutic targets.
