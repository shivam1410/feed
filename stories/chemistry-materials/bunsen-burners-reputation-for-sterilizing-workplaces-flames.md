A new study demonstrates that Bunsen burners, widely used in laboratories for nearly 150 years, do not effectively sterilize workbenches by wafting bacteria away as traditionally assumed. This finding challenges a practice taught in countless laboratories worldwide and raises questions about Bunsen burners' continued role in modern science. If the primary justification for their use—bacterial control—lacks empirical support, labs may need to reconsider their safety protocols or find alternative sterilization methods. The discovery highlights how long-standing laboratory practices can persist without rigorous scientific scrutiny.
