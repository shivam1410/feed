Experiments demonstrate that spontaneously charged water drops damage protective coatings on metal surfaces, initiating corrosion of the underlying metal. When water droplets develop a charge through contact or evaporation, they create electrochemical conditions at the coating-metal interface that accelerate degradation. This mechanism expands understanding of atmospheric corrosion beyond conventional wet-dry cycling, suggesting that localized electrostatic charging in water droplets contributes to material degradation. The finding has implications for predicting corrosion in humid environments where droplets commonly form.
